# ANE cannot rescue an SSD-bound token

Apple's Neural Engine cannot speed up Veystrix's decode, and the freshly reconciled 2026 literature agrees with the repository's own closed decision rather than overturning it. On the fanless 16 GB M4 MacBook Air, **289 of 385 ms per token (75%) is SSD expert-read wait** that the ANE has no data path to accelerate — it shares the same unified memory and SSD subsystem as the GPU ([hybrid_feasibility.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/hybrid_feasibility.md)). Amdahl's law caps the entire prize: even magically eliminating *all* GPU and router work yields **385/289 = 1.33×**, and removing only the 51 ms router sync yields **385/334 = 1.15×**; the ANE cannot touch the other 75% ([internal_engine_map.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/internal_engine_map.md)). External measurements independently reinforce the in-repo finding that batch-1 decode never reaches the ANE, that its FP16 numerics are **not bit-identical** (a hard-rule disqualifier), and that its traffic slows bandwidth-bound GPU work by **3–17%** ([REPORT.md](../experiments/ane/REPORT.md)). The apparently positive 2026 results — NPUMoE, MacPaw, Orion, and the full-model ANE demos — target compute-bound prefill on larger, faster machines, report ratios to weaker baselines, or rely on private APIs, so none contradict a rejection on *this* hardware under *these* rules ([ane_llm_evidence.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/ane_llm_evidence.md)). The recommendation is to keep the ANE route closed, invest in the SSD expert-read path, and run no new benchmark without explicit permission.

## The bottleneck is the SSD, not the matrix math

The premise that ANE could "bring something to the equation" assumes the token is compute-bound. It is not. The recorded decode budget is **289 ms SSD expert-read wait, about 70 ms GPU compute, and 51 ms router synchronisation** per token, with the components overlapping and rounded in the source ([internal_engine_map.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/internal_engine_map.md)). A separate sustained measurement gives **742 MB read per token at ~2.79 GB/s ≈ 266 ms/token of I/O**, and even *perfect expert residency* would cap that execution path near 9 tok/s ([internal_engine_map.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/internal_engine_map.md)). Nemotron tells the same story at a smaller scale: roughly 120 ms/token, of which about 75 ms is expert wait, with 179 MB/token of physical SSD reads even at a 77% page-cache hit rate. The ANE is a fixed-function graph executor that reaches unified memory through IOSurfaces; it has **no independent path to the SSD**, so its much-advertised TOPS and efficiency are simply irrelevant to the dominant term ([ane_hardware.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/ane_hardware.md)).

| What ANE could theoretically remove | Time saved | New token time | Speedup ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| All GPU (70 ms) + all router sync (51 ms) | 121 ms | 289 ms | **1.33×** |
| Router sync only (51 ms) | 51 ms | 334 ms | **1.15×** |
| The 289 ms SSD read wait | **0 ms** | — | **1.00× (untouchable)** |

That table is the whole business case in three rows. Worse, the 51 ms "router sync" is almost certainly a **stall/wait, not 51 ms of compute** that an ANE kernel could replace, so even the 1.15× row is optimistic ([hybrid_feasibility.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/hybrid_feasibility.md)). In reality ANE would *add* cost: roughly **0.095 ms XPC+IOKit dispatch** and up to a **~2.3 ms IOSurface round-trip** per call, plus the measured 3–17% slowdown it imposes on bandwidth-bound GPU work of exactly Veystrix's decode shape. The net effect on this token is **neutral-to-negative**, not a speedup.

## 2026 measurements corroborate, not overturn, the in-repo rejection

The 2026 external corpus is large but mostly confirms the mechanisms the in-repo report measured on 7 November 2026. On hardware limits: the ANE is a **fixed-function graph engine with ~32 MB of on-chip SRAM**, a **0.095–0.23 ms dispatch floor**, 4D channels-first buffers, 64-byte alignment, baked compile-time weights, and no dynamic control flow or unstructured gather ([ane_hardware.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/ane_hardware.md)). Its "38 TOPS" M4 figure is **19 FP16 TFLOPS × 2 by convention**; INT8 is dequantized to FP16 before compute, and there is **no native INT4 MAC** — 4-bit is a weight-storage format, not a 4-bit multiply ([ane_hardware.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/ane_hardware.md)). On Core ML reality: the only supported route is `MLModelConfiguration.computeUnits`, placement is a *hint*, a single unsupported op silently pushes a whole subgraph to CPU, and `MLComputePlan` reports what is *eligible* rather than what actually runs ([ane_software_stack.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/ane_software_stack.md)). On full-model ANE: the most detailed public LLM benchmark found a complete Qwen3-4B FP16 model running **2.0–3.9× slower in decode and 2.9–15.9× slower in prefill on ANE than on the GPU** of an M5 Pro, and Orion measured GPT-2 decode at **170 tok/s on ANE versus 283 tok/s on CPU** because of the IOSurface round-trip ([ane_llm_evidence.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/ane_llm_evidence.md)). None of this contradicts the in-repo rejection; it explains it from first principles.

The results that look pro-ANE need careful handling because they are *not* claims about this workload. **NPUMoE** reports 1.32–5.55× lower prefill latency, 1.81–7.37× lower energy, and **<1.1% accuracy loss** — but it was measured on M2 Max/Ultra machines with 64–192 GB, targets **prefill not decode**, uses FP16, and compares against custom NPU/CPU fallback baselines rather than an optimised GPU; graphs larger than 1.2 GB fall back to CPU ([ane_llm_evidence.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/ane_llm_evidence.md)). **MacPaw** shows a 350M *batched encoder* router 2.9× faster and 6.3× lower energy than MLX, but that is a large fixed model running beside generation, not a one-token projection; its own data put ANE memory bandwidth at only **44–70% of the GPU's** ([hybrid_feasibility.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/hybrid_feasibility.md)). **Bryngelson's** rigorous characterisation finds 3.8–14.5× conv/energy wins on M1/M5, but also that the GPU is ~2× faster on large GEMM and that short-sequence attention favours ANE while long-context attention favours GPU ([ane_llm_evidence.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/ane_llm_evidence.md)). The reconciliation is therefore straightforward: the in-repo report was right about an **SSD-bound, batch-1, bit-identical MoE decode on a 16 GB M4 Air**, and the external positives are about **compute-bound, batched, FP16 or relaxed-accuracy prefill on other machines**. They are different points in the design space, not a refutation.

## Bit-exactness and bandwidth disqualify ANE for the hot path

Even if the latency math were more generous, Veystrix's hard rules would still stop ANE from touching any output-affecting computation. The quality policy requires **no precision loss and a bit-identical token digest**, with Tier A evidence meaning identical token ids, continuation state, positions and KV hashes, and a task-level pass never excusing a failed bit-identical gate ([internal_engine_map.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/internal_engine_map.md)). ANE computes in FP16 and its routing numerics matched the FP32 reference on only **98.8% of 2,048 random rows** in the in-repo test — explicitly "not exact, so it would change routing" ([REPORT.md](../experiments/ane/REPORT.md)). Independent work finds the same fragility: `cadamcat` reports Core AI's grouped 4-bit LUT path returning **wrong answers** (1,921 of 4,096 values wrong), an iOS 4-bit palettized preset failing ANE compile and silently falling back to GPU, and a QDQ scale bug producing **339% error in one MLP**; arXiv:2608.22110 shows fused RMSNorm is 100% ANE-eligible while an arithmetically identical decomposition is 0% ANE-eligible, proving placement is a property of expression, not semantics ([ane_llm_evidence.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/ane_llm_evidence.md)). A numerical path that is merely "close" is disqualified by construction.

The second disqualifier is structural: **ANE bakes weights at compile time** (~4.2 s full recompile for 60 kernels, ~119 compiles per process, first-call compile ~1,015 ms for 24 programs) and has ~32 MB of usable SRAM, while Veystrix streams roughly **480 experts per token** (48 layers × top-10) from SSD through a 349-slot pool ([ane_hardware.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/ane_hardware.md)). A rotating 4-bit expert set cannot be hosted by a compile-time-weight engine; Core ML also cannot consume the Q4 `gather_qmm` op the experts use, and dense offload would need a duplicate FP16 copy of the 3.4 GB dense core that simply does not fit in 16 GB ([REPORT.md](../experiments/ane/REPORT.md)). Finally, the in-repo overlap test measured ANE traffic adding **+3.5% at 200 Hz and +17.6% flat-out to bandwidth-bound GPU work**, while the ANE matmul itself slowed from 1.22 ms to 2.8–3.0 ms beside GPU load — a memory-controller contention effect that heterogeneous roofline modelling on other SoCs independently predicts for bandwidth-bound regimes ([hybrid_feasibility.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/hybrid_feasibility.md)).

## Correcting the premise before any integration work

Three premises in the original brief need correction, because each would change an integration design if taken literally. First, `MLComputeUnits` has **four cases — `.all`, `.cpuOnly`, `.cpuAndGPU`, `.cpuAndNeuralEngine`**; there is **no `.neuralEngine` and no `.gpuAndNeuralEngine`**, and the `.neuralEngine`-style pinning a design would assume does not exist ([ane_software_stack.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/ane_software_stack.md)). The related `MLComputeEngine` "custom-layer ANE" premise could not be verified from Apple docs and should be treated as unfounded. Second, public Core ML cannot be trusted to route to ANE: on the `AtomGradient` testbed running **macOS 26.3, `compute_units=ALL` routed to GPU with ANE power ≈ 0 W**, so any "ANE" result from that path must be read as GPU-vs-GPU until proven otherwise; the in-repo report itself only exercised `CPU_ONLY`, `CPU_AND_GPU` and `CPU_AND_NE`, never `ALL` ([ane_llm_evidence.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/ane_llm_evidence.md)). Third, the **M5 "Neural Accelerators" are GPU matrix-multiply units programmed through Metal 4, not the ANE**, require macOS 26.2+, and do not exist on the M4 Air; Core AI, the 2026 framework that finally gives explicit ANE placement, requires **macOS 27** ([ane_software_stack.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/ane_software_stack.md)). MLX itself will not use the ANE — issue #18 was closed `wontfix` because the API is closed — so there is no drop-in backend to enable ([ane_software_stack.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/ane_software_stack.md)).

Epistemic status matters here. The strongest 2026 sources are **single-author or small-team preprints and repos, not peer-reviewed**: Bryngelson labels every claim measured/decompile-derived/predicted but remains one author; NPUMoE's gains are unreplicated and are ratios to its own baselines; `cadamcat` and `AtomGradient` are single repositories with mutually inconsistent toolchain behaviour; the widely cited **~250 MB ANE package ceiling and error -14 come from an independent black-box book, not Apple documentation**; and Apple's "3.7 bits/weight with no accuracy loss" claim has **no public accuracy table** ([ane_llm_evidence.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/ane_llm_evidence.md)). By contrast, the in-repo numbers are directly measured on the exact target machine — but were taken with **random weights, on battery, in short loops, through Python coremltools marshalling, with no hardware energy counter** ([REPORT.md](../experiments/ane/REPORT.md)). The honest conclusion is that the in-repo rejection is well-supported for the mechanism and the hardware, while the negative latency/energy claims from external work should be treated as capability ceilings rather than forecasts for Veystrix.

## Keep the seams closed and spend the effort on SSD reads

The brief asked for the realistic integration seams in the `neural-engine` branch **if and only if there is a credible workload for ANE**. That condition is not met: there is no credible way for ANE to shorten an SSD-bound, bit-identical token, so a seam-by-seam integration plan would be premature engineering. For the record, the seams that *would* be involved are a new per-model toggle in `Settings.swift` threaded through `SettingsView.swift` into a new arg/env in `LaunchPlan.swift`, a consumer in the Python `veystrix.model_router`, the `Backend` protocol plus the hard-coded `build_backend()` switch, and a packaging change to copy, sign and manifest a `.mlmodelc` — none of which exists today ([internal_app_map.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/internal_app_map.md)). Even that path is blocked by policy: the project repeatedly states that **no model weights ship** in the repo, app or release artifacts, and a `.mlmodelc` containing weights would function as a checkpoint and require a legal/notice and ADR decision, not just a build-script edit ([internal_app_map.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/internal_app_map.md)). The only credible ANE role the literature supports is an **auxiliary fixed model** — a batched router, speculative drafter, embedding or safety classifier — but with 75% of the token already in SSD wait there is little GPU contention to relieve, and any output-affecting auxiliary model would still fail the bit-exact gate ([hybrid_feasibility.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/hybrid_feasibility.md)).

The safe next steps follow directly. **Keep ANE development closed** as `experiments/ane/REPORT.md` records, and do not open an ANE route predictor, prefetcher, or output-affecting ANE computation; this is consistent with the default bit-identical exact-speed profile just landed on `neural-engine`. **Redirect effort to the real bottleneck** — expert-read wait — using the patterns the literature validates: larger or better expert caching/pooling, prefetch and I/O pipelining, one compiled expert graph shared across experts with runtime weight binding, and speculative multi-token windows to amortise reads (the EStream/BigMoMo/EdgeMoE templates), all of which attack the 289 ms rather than the 70 ms ([ane_llm_evidence.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/ane_llm_evidence.md)). **Do not run any benchmark, sweep or hardware test without the user's explicit per-workload permission**, per `AGENT_INVARIANTS.md` and the measurement standard; the only optional experiment the in-repo report leaves open is a `sudo` powermetrics energy check and plugged-in timing repeat, and even the author says it is not expected to change the recommendation — and its scripts are no longer in the tree ([internal_engine_map.md](../research_notes/Apple%20Neural%20Engine%20integration/internal_engine_map.md)). If the user later wants to revisit ANE for a *non-output-affecting auxiliary* task, the correct gate is first to establish a measurable GPU-contention or energy problem, and second to confirm Core AI / macOS 27 availability on the target — not to start with a port.

## Conclusion

The deeper takeaway is that Apple's own strategic direction moved LLM matrix compute into the **GPU** (M5 Neural Accelerators and Metal 4), leaving the ANE as a low-power home for fixed auxiliary models; Veystrix's decode bottleneck is I/O, so neither the ANE nor Apple's newest GPU tensor units address it. The 2026 research does not reveal a missed opportunity — it explains why every mainstream runtime bypasses the ANE and why the one in-repo experiment was correctly stopped. Reopening this branch would mean fighting fixed shapes, compile-time weights, non-bit-exact FP16, and shared-bandwidth contention for a theoretical 1.33× that cannot be realised; the productive frontier is the SSD read path, where the measured headroom actually lives.
