# Apple Neural Engine (ANE) for Transformer / LLM Inference — Evidence Base

Scope: external research only, current as of Nov 2026. Every quantitative claim below is tagged with the chip it was measured on. "Measured" = instrumented result in a paper/repo; "claimed" = Apple/vendor claim or author assertion without supporting measurement in the source. Important caveat for the report writer: several of the richest 2026 sources (Bryngelson ANE guide, ANEForge, Orion, the `cadamcat` and `AtomGradient` repos) are independent/single-author or small-team works posted to arXiv/GitHub, not peer-reviewed; one repo (`AtomGradient`) reports that on its macOS 26.3 build `compute_units=ALL` actually routes to GPU, so "ANE" results via public Core ML need skeptical reading. I flag this throughout.

## Q1. Academic papers: transformer/LLM inference on ANE or Apple Silicon, with engine comparisons (throughput / latency / energy)

### Takeaway
ANE delivers strong FP16 throughput and 2–14× better energy efficiency than the GPU for convolution-like and mid-size GEMM work, but for complete autoregressive LLMs the GPU is usually faster in both prefill and decode; ANE's energy advantage survives on short prefill. Fixed-function, 16-core-class ANEs peak around 12–19 TFLOP/s FP16 (vs Apple's 15.8 TOPS A15 / 38 TOPS M4 INT8 spec, which dequantizes to FP16 before compute).

### Cited Findings
- **Bryngelson, "Apple Neural Engine: Architecture, Programming, and Performance", arXiv:2606.22283 (v1 Jun 2026, v2 Oct 2026)** — the most thorough reverse-engineering/characterization; author explicitly labels every claim measured (M1/H13 and M5/H17s silicon), decompile-derived, or predicted. Roofline on **M1**: ~12 FP16 TFLOP/s compute, ~85 GB/s DRAM bandwidth, ridge point ~141 FLOP/byte, 2 MB on-chip working-set threshold, **0.23 ms floor per dispatch**, ~0.37 pJ/FLOP at compute optimum (~0.5 pJ/FLOP sustained). A 3×3 conv at 256 channels runs ~3.8× faster and ~9× more energy-efficient than the same chip's GPU; a 16-layer conv stack reaches **2063 GFLOP/s/W on ANE vs 142 on GPU = 14.5×** on M1; M5 2289 vs 175 = 13×; M2 2234.6 vs 172.8 = 12.9×. Large 4096-inner-dim GEMM: GPU ~2× faster in raw throughput but ANE uses 4.4 W vs GPU 32.5 W (4× efficiency edge). Power: ~0 mW idle (rail-off, five gated domains on M1), dispatch floor ~0.9 W, compute-bound 1024×4096 FP16 GEMM ~4.3 W, INT8 form ~5.8 W (M1 Max). Thermal: flat power/throughput over a 176 s saturating loop, no local DVFS, fixed operating point across 56,527 dispatches. Encoder-serving crossover: ~batch 23 for single-sentence encoder, ~batch 6 for self-attention block beyond which GPU wins. Workload fit summary: ANE-favored = conv stacks, vision backbones, short-sequence attention, mid-size resident matmul; GPU-favored = large square GEMM, long-sequence attention, autoregressive decode; CPU/fallback = tiny ops below dispatch floor, unsupported ops, dynamic control flow. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22283v2); [readthedocs guide, ch.10](https://ane-guide.readthedocs.io/en/latest/part-3-performance/10-power-efficiency.html); [summary](https://ark.marianposaceanu.com/apple-neural-engine-architecture-performance)
- **Orion (arXiv:2603.06728, Mar 2026, UVA-affiliated author)** — measured on **M4 Max** (H16, 16 ANE cores). Table 1: Apple INT8 spec 38 TOPS but ANE dequantizes INT8→FP16 before compute, so measured peak ~19 TFLOP/s FP16; 32 MB on-chip SRAM, ~30% throughput drop when working set exceeds it; dispatch overhead ~0.095 ms; ~119 compilations/process limit. 1×1 conv is ~3× faster than equivalent matmul; deep 16–64-op graphs reach ~94% ANE utilization vs ~30% for single ops. GPT-2 124M inference: ANE decode 170 tok/s (5.76 ms/tok), **CPU decode 283 tok/s (3.48 ms/tok) — CPU wins single-token decode** due to ~2.3 ms IOSurface round-trip per dispatch; ANE prefill cached 165 tok/s, first call 12 tok/s due to ~1015 ms compile of 24 programs. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06728)
- **"Efficient Mixture-of-Experts LLM Inference with Apple Silicon NPUs" (NPUMoE), arXiv:2604.18788, Apr 2026 (UVA)** — measured on **M2 Max (16-core ANE, 64 GB)** and **M2 Ultra (32-core ANE, 192 GB)**. Dense matmul microbenchmark on M2 Ultra: ANE 1.4–2.3× lower runtime than CPU, but only for mid-size (M≈256–2048; benefit shrinks at M=64 and at M=32048/vocab size). CPU–NPU synchronization can exceed 60% of runtime worst case. Prefill dominates long-context inference (claimed 80% of latency, 70% of CPU cycles). Headline: vs its three baselines, prefill latency 1.32–5.55× lower, energy 1.81–7.37× lower, CPU cycles 1.78–5.54× lower, <1.1% accuracy loss vs FP16. (These ratios are vs custom NPU/CPU fallback baselines, NOT vs an optimized GPU baseline.) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18788)
- **"A comparative performance and efficiency analysis of Apple's M architectures: A GEMM case study", Future Generation Computer Systems, Jul 2026 (open access)** — M2 Pro–M4 Pro. AMX (CPU matrix unit) best for FP32/FP64; on M4 Pro AMX reaches 68% of GPU FP32 perf at 42% of its power; ANE limited to FP16 but >700 GFLOP/s/W under batched workloads. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167739X26000270)
- **"Evaluation of Domain-Specific Architectures for General-Purpose Applications in Apple Silicon", arXiv:2511.13450** — M1 vs M4: ANE up to 3.8 TFLOP/s on M4 Pro (GPU 4.7 TFLOP/s same SoC) for adapted GEMM, with 5.2 W on ANE vs 24 W on GPU. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.13450)
- **"Apple vs. Oranges: Evaluating the Apple Silicon M-Series SoCs for HPC Performance and Efficiency", arXiv:2502.05317 (KTH, Feb 2025)** — M1/M2/M3/M4, ANE supports INT8 and FP16, 16 cores on each base chip; benchmarks YOLO v3/v11 and GEMM/Jacobi/Multigrid across engines. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05317v1)
- **"Benchmarking and Characterization of Large Language Model Inference on Apple Silicon", POMACS 9(3) art.48, Dec 2025 / SIGMETRICS 2026 (Benazir & Lin, UVA)** — peer-reviewed LLM inference characterization on Apple Silicon (GPU/CPU focus; same group as NPUMoE). — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3771563)
- **"Benchmarking On-Device Machine Learning on Apple Silicon with MLX", arXiv:2510.18921** — **M1**: matmul 26.19 ms (M1) vs 3.96 ms on CUDA A10; linear 18.88 vs 3.11 ms; softmax 27.91 vs 1.06 ms. MLX GPU vs M1 CPU speedups e.g. BERT-large 321–365%, RoBERTa-base up to 944%. These are GPU/CPU, not ANE, but give M1 baseline context. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18921)
- **Kasperek et al., Sensors 2022 (M1/M1 Pro/M1 Max) and Sensors 2023 (M1/M2)** — using Apple Create ML: "advantage of the Neural Engine is clearly visible, giving a huge boost on all M1 based machines" for text/vision/tabular tasks; little difference between M1/M1 Pro/M1 Max. — [MDPI 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/20/8005); [MDPI 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/23/12/5424)
- Specific paper named in the brief, "Power and Performance Analysis of ML inference on Apple M1": I could not locate a paper under that exact title. Closest matches are the Bryngelson guide, the M4 GEMM study, Apple-vs-Oranges, and the Sensors Create ML papers above. Treat the exact-title item as unverified.

### Inferences
- ANE is a roofline/bandwidth machine: it wins when work is fused, resident in ≤2 MB (direct path) / ≤32 MB SRAM (M4), static-shaped and compute-dense; it loses for bandwidth-bound single-token GEMV and long-sequence attention.
- Energy, not speed, is ANE's differentiator. For any pipeline decision "offload X to ANE", expect ~2–14× energy benefit per op but not a latency win over GPU except for conv-like/mid-size GEMM and short prefill.
- Public-coremltools LLM paths in 2025–2026 tend to be "compute_units=ALL is a hint"; measured placements diverge, so any claim must be verified with power counters or device plans.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed end-to-end token/s number for a 4-bit MoE decode/prefill on ANE (NPUMoE is closest but reports ratios to its own baselines, and the detailed per-model token rates were in the truncated part of the paper not retrieved).
- No Mamba/SSM scan-on-ANE measurements found anywhere; selective scan appears fundamentally unsuited to a static-shape compile-once NPU but this is inference, not a cited result.

## Q2. Apple's own research: foundation models, LoRA/adapters, and apple/ml-ane-transformers

### Takeaway
Apple's own evidence says ANE-friendly transformer restructuring gives up to 10× lower latency / 14× lower peak memory (DistilBERT case study), and that Apple Intelligence's 3B on-device model runs at 30 tok/s on iPhone 15 Pro using mixed 2-bit/4-bit palettization averaging 3.7 bits/weight with LoRA adapters — but Apple never publicly attributes foundation-model speed to ANE specifically, and never published MoE-ANE results.

### Cited Findings
- **"Deploying Transformers on the Apple Neural Engine" (machinelearning.apple.com, Jun 6 2022)** — ANE history/specs: A11 (iPhone X, 2017) 0.6 TFLOP/s FP16; 5th-gen 16-core ANE (A15, 2021) 15.8 TFLOP/s = 26×. Four optimization principles: (1) use **(B,C,1,S) 4D channels-first** layout — swap all `nn.Linear` for `nn.Conv2d` (1×1 convs), because ANE's last axis is unpacked, contiguous, 64-byte aligned; (2) **chunk large intermediates** — split Q/K/V into explicit per-head attention functions for L2 residency; (3) **minimize memory copies** — avoid reshapes, one transpose of K, use einsum `bchq,bkhc->bkhq` for SDPA to avoid transpose/reshape; (4) **handle bandwidth-boundness** — latency is ~constant for seq len 32/64/128 at batch 1 while compute quadruples; increase batch or shrink weights via quantization/pruning. — [Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/neural-engine-transformers)
- **Same source, measured numbers** — DistilBERT on **iPhone 13 ANE, iOS 16**: up to **10× faster forward pass and 14× lower peak memory** vs baseline; at seq 128 / batch 1, **3.47 ms at 0.454 W** and **9.44 ms at 0.072 W**; tutorial config gives only **2.84×** latency improvement (the 10× figure is at seq 512 / batch 8). 606 ops in optimized model, of which **4 of 606 run on CPU — the embedding lookups**, which are more efficient there. Load/compile times increase due to higher op count (one-time cost). Peak ANE throughput "far from saturated" for this config. For comparison, Apple cites HuggingFace/AWS 5–6 ms latency for same model on AWS Inferentia server ASIC. — [Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/neural-engine-transformers); [GitHub README](https://github.com/apple-aiml-research/ml-ane-transformers); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/ane-transformers)
- **"Introducing Apple's On-Device and Server Foundation Models" (Jun 10, 2024)** — ~3B on-device model; grouped-query attention, shared input/output vocab embedding (49K on-device, 100K server). **Low-bit palletization** is "a critical optimization technique" for memory/power/performance. Mixed **2-bit and 4-bit** LoRA-adapter configuration averaging **3.7 bits per weight** to match uncompressed accuracy; can go to **3.5 bits/weight without significant quality loss**. Also activation quantization, embedding quantization, and "an approach to enable efficient KV cache update on our neural engines". **Measured on iPhone 15 Pro: TTFT ~0.6 ms per prompt token, generation 30 tokens/s**, before token speculation. Uses Talaria latency/power tool for per-op bitrate selection. Adapters modify attention matrices, attention projection matrix, and point-wise FFN fully-connected layers. — [Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/introducing-apple-foundation-models)
- **"Apple Intelligence Foundation Language Models", arXiv:2407.21075 (Jul 2024)** — AFM-on-device: dense decoder-only, shared input/output embedding, pre-norm RMSNorm, query/key normalization, **GQA with 8 KV heads**, SwiGLU, RoPE base 500k; dim 3072, head dim 128, 24 query heads, 26 layers, 2.58B non-embedding + 0.15B embedding params. Distilled and pruned from a 6.4B model; pretrained 6.3T tokens. LoRA adapters with quantized low-bit rates, on-device adapters "on the order of 10 MB". Architecture diagram shows both device and server SoCs containing a Neural Engine. No ANE-specific throughput numbers in the paper. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21075v2); [PDF](https://machinelearning.apple.com/papers/apple_intelligence_foundation_language_models.pdf)
- **"Deploying Attention-Based Vision Transformers to Apple Neural Engine" (Apple ML Research, Jan 5 2024)** — further ANE ViT work (tensor-rank limits, NHWC layout for window partition/reverse); relevant to attention layout choices but no LLM numbers retrieved. — linked from [Apple transformer article](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/neural-engine-transformers)
- Trail of Bits analysis notes the server model is "likely" a large MoE (speculation, not Apple-confirmed) — do not treat as fact. — [Trail of Bits](https://blog.trailofbits.com/2024/06/14/understanding-apples-on-device-and-server-foundations-model-release)

### Inferences
- Apple's own transformer-to-ANE recipe is essentially "turn GEMMs into 1×1 convs and split attention per head into static chunks"; this maps well to FFN/expert GEMMs but the embedding lookup stays on CPU and fixed shapes are mandatory.
- The only published Apple evidence of 4-bit on ANE is indirect: palettization is described as critical for the on-device model, but Apple does not state which engine executes which bit-rate ops. The "3.7 bpw with no accuracy loss" number is a system-level claim tied to LoRA mixed-precision, not an ANE microbenchmark.
- Apple's success uses a sliding/fixed-size KV cache on neural engines (per its blog), consistent with practitioners' findings (Q3).

### Gaps
- Apple never published: (a) which transformer sublayers ran on ANE vs GPU in the 3B model, (b) per-layer latency/energy, (c) any MoE-on-ANE data, (d) whether 2-bit vs 4-bit ops were assigned to different engines.
- No Apple-published INT4 accuracy table for LLMs beyond "3.5 bpw without significant quality loss".

## Q3. Real projects running LLaMA / Qwen / small LLMs via Core ML on ANE: sizes, quantization, token rates, failure modes (incl. MoE)

### Takeaway
Complete quantized LLMs on ANE through public toolchains are rarely faster than GPU; the best-measured small-model result is ~14.6 tok/s ANE decode for Qwen3-4B FP16 on an M5 Pro (GPU 30.1 tok/s), while genuine ANE batch dispatch via private API can hit 268 tok/s prefill on a 0.8B model. MoE-specific ANE work exists in research (NPUMoE) but essentially not in hobbyist projects.

### Cited Findings
- **`cadamcat/llms-on-apple-neural-engine` (M5 Pro, 48 GiB, macOS 27.0, Core AI / MLX 0.32.2 / coremltools 9.0)** — the most detailed public LLM-on-ANE benchmark, with bundled reproducible records. Complete **Qwen3-4B FP16** via Core AI: GPU faster at every input 500–16K — 2.0–3.9× in decode, 2.9–15.9× in prefill. At 1K input: prefill 887.7 tok/s ANE vs 3177.1 tok/s GPU (3.58×); decode 14.56 tok/s ANE from 1K KV vs 30.09 tok/s GPU. ANE prefill projection work 1.7–7.0 TFLOP/s, GPU 20.5–27.2 TFLOP/s. Model-based decode read rate: GPU 227–250 GB/s, ANE 59–122 GB/s. Energy: for 500–2K prefill ANE uses 0.68–0.81× the GPU's component energy per input token; 4K–16K 0.90–1.33×; decode 0.86–1.18×. Graph sizing matters: vs the earlier 256/2K/32K graph ladder, a matched graph makes ANE decode 1.80–4.40× faster from 2K (3.11→13.70 tok/s) and prefill 2.76–6.40× faster from 4K. — [GitHub README](https://github.com/cadamcat/llms-on-apple-neural-engine)
- **Same repo — quantization failure modes (M5 Pro)**: iOS 4-bit palettized preset → ANE compile fails silently, graph runs on GPU at 2.87 tok/s decode from 1K (FP16 on ANE 5.1–8.1× faster); grouped 4-bit via Core AI native LUT path is accepted, shows ANE activity, but returns **wrong answers** (1921/4096 values wrong, predictably); W4A16 served from native ANE host ~0.248× GPU speed at 1024 positions (0.25× in summary); W8A8 synthetic control gives 1.86–1.87× over FP16 baseline through Core AI; real Gemma-4-E4B QAT A8W4 first MLP is "wrong, and not faster" — a QDQ multiply on ANE dequantizes using another QDQ's scale (339% off in one MLP) in both Core AI and Core ML. One- and two-position decode queries fail with `0xe00002c2`; 4- and 8-position work; 4 positions gives only 1.001–1.008× speed change. ANE compiler service can hold 178.4 GiB of deleted compile inputs until it exits; Python binding leaked 1.875 MiB/call, native Swift hosts did not. — [GitHub README](https://github.com/cadamcat/llms-on-apple-neural-engine)
- **`AtomGradient/hybrid-ane-mlx-bench` (M2 Ultra 192 GB, 800 GB/s, macOS 26.3)** — **Core ML `compute_units=ALL` routes to GPU, not ANE (ANE power ≈ 0 W)**; so "hybrid" CoreML results are GPU-vs-GPU. Genuine ANE batch dispatch via private API gives **268 tok/s prefill for Qwen3.5-0.8B, 11.3× over sequential dispatch**; ANE-LM sequential prefill + GPU decode gives 17,831 ms TTFT at 410 tokens vs GPU 96 ms; ANE batch (32 tok) brings TTFT to ~276 ms. ANE prefill reduces GPU power from 62.05 W to 0.22 W (282×). MLX GPU decode: Qwen3.5-0.8B FP16 69–72 tok/s; 2B 8-bit 139–142; 2B BF16 100–101; 9B 8-bit 56–57. Hybrid mixed-precision cache bridge (FP16 CoreML prefill → 8-bit MLX decode) causes 11–16% decode degradation. — [GitHub README](https://github.com/AtomGradient/hybrid-ane-mlx-bench)
- **`anentropic/experiments-coreml-ane-distilbert` (M1 Mac)** — CoreML ANE ~4.4 ms/inference vs PyTorch CPU ~44 ms (~10×); ANE power spikes 1.3–2.8 W observed. (Independent replication of Apple's DistilBERT claim.) — [GitHub](https://github.com/anentropic/experiments-coreml-ane-distilbert)
- **Stephen Panaro, "Inside Apple's 2023 Transformer Models" (blog, measured experience)** — ANE requires fixed input/output sizes so a traditional KV cache is off the table; Apple uses a sliding fixed-size KV cache ("2 new tokens + cache of 3 tokens" always same shape). Reports 2–5× speedup in his experience; MUST use IOSurface-backed inputs/outputs or copy costs erase gains; on Sonoma/iOS17 CPU segments at the start of a model cause a severe slowdown (filed as a regression) — put CPU work in a separate model. — [Blog](https://stephenpanaro.com/blog/inside-apples-2023-transformers.html)
- **MoE via public projects**: no hobbyist Core ML repo with a working MoE (Mixtral/Qwen-MoE) on ANE found. **NPUMoE** (Q3/Q7) is the only direct Apple-NPU MoE system: Phi-3.5-MoE (42.6B, 16 experts, top-k=2, 83.75 GB, each expert 150 MB), Phi-tiny-MoE (3.8B, 7.51 GB), Qwen3-30B-A3B (48 layers, 128 experts, 28.64 GB) on M2 Max/M2 Ultra. Uses ANEMLL library + Core ML Swift. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18788)
- **Non-Apple NPU MoE comparators (patterns relevant to Veystrix's problem, not ANE measurements)**: EStream (Snapdragon, 7B–16B MoEs, 256–4096-token prompts) uses one compiled expert graph shared across experts with runtime route/weight-address binding, UFS-flash expert virtualization and UFS–NPU pipelining; 2.25–27.57× TTFT speedup and 1.19–12.29× lower peak memory. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06551v1). BigMoMo (Qualcomm Hexagon) uses speculative-decoding multi-token windows to batch expert weight movement and overlap NPU compute with flash loads. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14643v2). EdgeMoE (IEEE TMC 2025) keeps non-expert weights in device memory, experts on external storage with expert-wise bitwidth adaptation + preloading. — [IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10906629). Mixture-of-Cache-Conditional-Experts (arXiv:2412.00099) shows MoEs tolerate lossy cache-aware reranking: >50% cache-miss reduction, 0.1–3% perplexity increase, <0.1% downstream accuracy loss, up to 2× speedup over LRU on mobile. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.00099)

### Inferences
- For a 4-bit MoE decode pipeline, ANE is unlikely to beat a good Metal/MLX GPU decode path today; the realistic win is offloading static dense expert FFN GEMMs (and attention projections) during **prefill**, keeping router/top-k/scatter and sampling on CPU — exactly NPUMoE's partition.
- Batch size, not model precision alone, determines whether ANE is utilized: single-token dispatch is dominated by a 0.23 ms floor (M1) / 2.3 ms IOSurface round-trip (Orion, M4 Max) / graph-launch serialization.
- Multi-position (4 or 8 token) decode calls are a practical way to amortize ANE dispatch; 1–2 position queries fail on the tested M5 Pro toolchain.
- Graph capacity must be sized per input length; fixed 2K/32K graphs penalize short inputs badly.

### Gaps
- No reproducible public result for Mixtral/Qwen-MoE run through Core ML on ANE (successful or failed) outside NPUMoE's paper.
- Exact NPUMoE per-model latencies/energy in absolute units were beyond the retrieved portion of the paper (section 5 tables); only relative ranges were captured.
- No data on 4-bit MoE decode on ANE at all — the `cadamcat` repo explicitly says "decode with 4-bit weights on ANE is still unmeasured."

## Q4. Which layers run well vs poorly on ANE (attention/SDPA, softmax, layernorm, GEMM, embedding, MoE router, gather/scatter, Mamba/SSM)

### Takeaway
ANE excels at fused FP16 convolutions/1×1-conv GEMMs, large-vocab classifier matmuls, and softmax; it is poor or unsupported for embeddings, top-k/argmax routing, scatter/gather, dynamic indexing, masking, layernorm/normalization backwards, and single-token decode. Causal SDPA masks are silently ignored on the direct path.

### Cited Findings
- **Orion per-operation measurements, Stories110M on M4 Max** (Table 11): classifier forward (embedding×x) CPU 10.77 ms → ANE 1.06 ms = **10.2×**; softmax over vocab=32000 CPU 81.11 ms → ANE 2.40 ms = **33.8×**; RMSNorm backward CPU 0.18 ms → ANE 0.21 ms ≈ **1× (no benefit)**. CPU/ANE division of labor (Table 5): ANE does transformer fwd/bwd (dx); **CPU** does token sampling, Adam optimizer, ∇W accumulation, NLL loss+gradient ("gather not in MIL"), classifier backward (32K channels rejected), embedding lookup (table indexing). Constraint list: `concat` op rejected; SDPA causal masks silently ignored (manual masking needed); `gelu` invalid (tanh approx required); conv has no bias parameter; 32K-channel convolutions rejected; multi-input/output surfaces must be uniform size and are bound alphabetically by name; minimum IOSurface ~49 KB (single-token [1,768,1,1] must be padded to [1,768,1,16]). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06728)
- **ANEForge (arXiv:2606.17090, M5 Pro + M1 Max/M2 Pro)**: native fused-attention layer; operator census 116/166 MIL operators, 26 native layers below public compiler; per-program floor ~74 µs (M5 Pro); a memory-bound op finishes sooner on CPU below 64 KiB and on GPU above 512 KiB; engine advantage comes from fusion (128-op program pays the floor once). Measured across generations: GEMM floor (M=64,K=256,N=256) 0.164 ms M1 Max / 0.110 M2 Pro / 0.092 M5 Pro; conv stack 1.918/1.265/0.757 ms; **attention (S=197,D=768,12 heads) 0.613/0.589/0.230 ms**; **layer norm (197×768) 0.321/0.283/0.195 ms**; ResNet-18 fwd 1.064 ms (M2 Pro) / 0.330 ms (M5 Pro); ResNet-18 ~9× less energy than GPU at matched precision (paired runs 5.5–12.7×). Single hardware lane per tested machine — no concurrent dispatch; gains come from fusing into one program. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17090v2)
- **Apple's transformer principles (Q2)** — embedding lookups kept on CPU (4 of 606 DistilBERT ops); per-head split attention + einsum avoids reshape/transpose; 1×1 conv replaces Linear. — [Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/neural-engine-transformers)
- **NPUMoE operator partitioning (M2 Max/Ultra)** — on ANE: attention Q/K/V projections, softmax, stateful KV-cache update, expert FFN (static shapes); on CPU: layerNorm, masking, **expert routing (top-k/argmax), token dispatch/merge (scatter, gather, weighted combine)** due to dynamic indexing/irregular memory access. NPU supports static dense operators and conv2D but "cannot perform arbitrary runtime dependent core MoE operations such as dynamic indexing, tensor reshaping, top-k (router)". Overflow tokens cannot be spilled dynamically to CPU because Core ML fixes device placement at compile time. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18788)
- **Bryngelson guide** — operation-by-device matrix (appendix A) distinguishes advertised vs actually-executable ops (e.g. 3D conv never lowers to engine); short-sequence attention favors ANE, long-sequence attention favors GPU; decoder/embedding/router-type ops fall to CPU/fallback. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22283v2)
- **"How Weight Encoding Affects Language Model Placement and Performance on the ANE" (arXiv:2608.22110, M1/M3)** — **fused RMSNorm is 100% ANE-eligible while arithmetically identical subln decomposition is 0% (CPU-only)** — placement is a property of expression, not semantics. Heavily-grouped GQA (2 KV heads) is the smaller operator on ANE (~2.5 d²) vs LIV conv (~4 d²), and the one admitted at fp16. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22110)
- **Sparse ternary GEMM on M1 CPU (arXiv:2510.06957, context for gather/scatter)** — "scalar implementation achieves better performance than vectorized on Apple Silicon due to the absence of scatter/gather instructions." — CPU AMX result, not ANE. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06957v1)

### Inferences
- For a 4-bit MoE decode pipeline, the ANE-viable pieces are: static expert FFN GEMMs (as 1×1 convs), QKV/output projections, large-vocab softmax, and KV-cache update with fixed shapes. The router (top-k), gather/scatter, embeddings, sampling and normalization are CPU-side — which is also NPUMoE's published conclusion.
- Per-layer decision should be roofline-based: if the layer's working set is small enough and the batch/launch work clears the dispatch floor, ANE; otherwise GPU/CPU. Fusion matters more than per-op speed.
- Attention's ANE advantage is real at moderate sequence lengths (197-token attention 0.23 ms on M5 Pro) but reverses for long contexts (cadamcat: ANE falls to 1.7 TFLOP/s vs GPU 20–27).

### Gaps
- No measured numbers for Mamba/SSM selective scan on ANE; no measured numbers for store pos-Norm residual stream or expert-gather with FP16 on ANE beyond NPUMoE's aggregate results.
- Per-layer numbers come from different papers/chips and are not directly comparable.

## Q5. Quantization requirements: palettized/affine weights for speed, FP16-on-ANE vs GPU-FP16, INT4 support and accuracy loss

### Takeaway
On the **public Core ML path**, weight encoding gates accelerator placement: FP16 exports of small LMs can be assigned entirely to CPU, while INT8/2-bit palettized versions get ~83–99% ANE residency and run 1.8–2.2× faster. ANE hardware itself computes FP16 (INT8 is dequantized to FP16, saving bandwidth only); "INT4" is supported as a lookup-table/palettized weight format with severe correctness/performance pitfalls in current toolchains. Apple's own model uses 3.7 bpw average with no measured accuracy loss claim backed by a public table.

### Cited Findings
- **arXiv:2608.22110 "What actually runs / How Weight Encoding Affects..." (M1 + M3)**: five matched checkpoints. On **M1** a 25.85M-parameter conv-heavy FP16 model is assigned 0/331 ops to ANE with zero measured memory-controller traffic; same graph in INT8 or 2-bit returns to ~83% residency and runs 1.8–2.2× faster; a 22.29M all-attention FP16 model sits at 98.9% ANE. Specific warm latency table (M1): q25-fp16 CPU+ANE 1.454 ms, q25-int8 0.770 ms, q25-ternary 0.638 ms (i.e. ~1.9–2.3×); q50-fp16 2.787→2.079 ms, q50-ternary 0.909 ms. **Decode cost = bytes streamed per token**, at a constant ~0.77 fraction of nominal encoding width: 1.539 / 0.753 / 0.197 B/param at fp16 / int8 / 2-bit (nominal 2/1/0.25). On **M3** q25-fp16 shows 0 mJ ANE energy and 1.125 ms prediction; q25-int8 shows 2200 mJ ANE reading and 0.645 ms; CPU-only int8 1.130 ms. Smallest/fastest models are ternary: 25M ternary 10.0–10.8 MB, 0.62–0.64 ms/token; 50M 16.8 MB, 0.86 ms — 9.8×/6.1× smaller and 3.0×/2.2× faster than the conv-heavy fp16 start. Design procedure: choose encoding first, then spend byte budget on parameters. Limitations: fixed seq-1 input, single M1 session, no sustained thermal/stateful generation. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22110); [v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.22110v1)
- **INT8 is dequantized to FP16 on ANE** (Orion, M4 Max; "38 TOPS INT8 spec" debunked → ~19 TFLOP/s FP16 actual; INT8 saves memory bandwidth only, not compute cycles). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06728)
- **FP16 on ANE vs GPU**: Bryngelson (M1) — GPU ~2× faster in throughput on a 4096-inner-dim GEMM, but ANE 4.4 W vs 32.5 W (4× efficiency). ANE and CPU/GPU share DRAM, and at long sequences GPU wins outright. — [readthedocs ch.10](https://ane-guide.readthedocs.io/en/latest/part-3-performance/10-power-efficiency.html)
- **INT4 / palettization success and failure on M5 Pro (`cadamcat`)**: Core AI native LUT grouped 4-bit accepted and shows real ANE activity but returns wrong answers; iOS 4-bit palettized preset fails ANE compile → silent GPU fallback (2.87 tok/s); direct grouped-scale Core ML INT4 graph is numerically correct but CPU-selected — "ANE only support per-cout/per-tensor quantization"; K32 decomposition costs ~4× in a synthetic ablation; Core ML four-bit palette graphs run at 0.065–0.280× Core AI speed; saved as direct INT4 the same codes run 0.952–0.985× Core AI four-bit speed; on one E4B projection+MLP, Core AI four-bit palette weights on ANE ran **1.465–3.175× faster than FP16**. W4A16 1.86–1.87× gain only on a 128-layer synthetic chain; 0.86× at 1 layer; exact-zero weights make FP16 itself 1.88× faster. — [GitHub README](https://github.com/cadamcat/llms-on-apple-neural-engine)
- **Apple Intelligence accuracy claim**: mixed 2-bit/4-bit palettization averaging 3.7 bits/weight "achieves the same accuracy as the uncompressed models"; 3.5 bpw "without significant quality loss" (LoRA mixed-precision framework + Talaria per-op bitrate selection). No public accuracy table. — [Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/introducing-apple-foundation-models)
- Apple's ane_transformers authors explicitly say ANE peak throughput was far from saturated for DistilBERT and that quantization/pruning could improve it further (2022). — [Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/neural-engine-transformers)

### Inferences
- For ANE on the public Core ML path, "use palettized/affine quantized weights" is effectively a prerequisite for the compiler to route the graph to ANE for small LMs — FP16 does not guarantee ANE residency. Quantization primarily changes bytes/token (decode is bandwidth-bound), so 2-bit/4-bit palettization is the lever for speed as well as memory.
- Hardware FP16 rate is the real compute ceiling even when weights are INT4/INT8; quantization buys bandwidth, not MACs — so offloading decode to ANE yields at best a bandwidth-limited improvement.
- Current 4-bit toolchain correctness is fragile (wrong answers from LUT paths, QDQ scale bug, silent GPU fallback); any 4-bit MoE on ANE must validate outputs numerically against CPU reference, not just measure speed.
- 3.7 bpw is achievable at parity only with Apple's LoRA mixed-precision + calibration approach; no evidence a naive uniform INT4 keeps quality.

### Gaps
- No measured perplexity/accuracy delta for INT4 on a small LLM running specifically on ANE (vs CPU/GPU) outside Apple's aggregate 3.5/3.7 bpw claim.
- Unclear whether Apple's 3.5 bpw used ANE at all for those ops.

## Q6. Known limitations practitioners document (unsupported ops, fixed shapes, recompilation, load times, thermal, memory)

### Takeaway
ANE is a compile-then-dispatch fixed-shape engine: weights are baked at compile time, every shape/length change can force recompilation, op coverage is narrower than MIL's vocabulary, and single-token/nested dispatch plus CPU↔ANE copying can erase speedups. Thermal throttling is largely a non-issue (flat power, rail-off idle); disk/memory leaks and toolchain defects are the bigger practical hazards.

### Cited Findings
- **Fixed shapes / KV cache**: ANE requires fixed input/output sizes; practitioners use a sliding fixed-size KV cache and IOSurface-backed I/O, reporting 2–5× speedup but note copy costs erase it and Sonoma/iOS17 has a CPU-segment-at-start regression. — [Panaro blog](https://stephenpanaro.com/blog/inside-apples-2023-transformers.html)
- **Baked weights & recompilation (Orion, M4 Max)**: weights embedded at compile time; naïve training requires full recompile ~4.2 s/step for 60 kernels (83.9% of wall time), ~119 compilations per process before silent failure, ~1015 ms first-call compile of 24 GPT-2 programs; delta reload (unload → patch BLOBFILEs → reload, no ANECCompile) cuts 4200→494 ms (8.5×), 3.8× total training speedup; exec() restart costs ~50 ms. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06728)
- **Op/layout restrictions (Orion, M4 Max)**: concat rejected; SDPA causal masks silently ignored; GELU invalid; conv bias unsupported; 32K-channel conv rejected; ~49 KB minimum IOSurface; uniform-size multi-input/output buffers bound alphabetically; packed [1,C,1,S] buffer reads; ~30% throughput drop beyond 32 MB SRAM; fp16 overflow cascade requires activation clamping ±65504 for training. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06728)
- **Dispatch floor and working set (Bryngelson, M1/M5)**: 0.23 ms floor per dispatch (M1), ~74 µs (ANEForge M5 Pro), 2 MB working-set threshold on the direct path, ridge 141 FLOP/byte — small or memory-bound layers lose to CPU/GPU. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22283v2); [ANEForge](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17090v2)
- **Toolchain/compilation failures (`cadamcat`, M5 Pro)**: 1- and 2-position decode queries error `0xe00002c2`; palettized 4-bit preset silently falls back to GPU; QDQ multiply uses wrong scale (339% error in one MLP); ANE compiler service holds deleted compile inputs (178.4 GiB in one run); Python binding leaks 1.875 MiB/call; graph >1.2 GB (M2 Ultra) falls back to CPU (NPUMoE). — [GitHub README](https://github.com/cadamcat/llms-on-apple-neural-engine); [NPUMoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18788)
- **Load/compile time (Apple)**: optimized DistilBERT has 606 ops vs baseline, increasing load and compile times (one-time; load asynchronously). — [GitHub README](https://github.com/apple-aiml-research/ml-ane-transformers)
- **Memory pressure**: AFM-server-style large models rely on unified memory; M2 Ultra 192 GB holds 83.75 GB PhiMoE fully resident; mobile NPUs cannot hold all experts, requiring UFS flash paging (EStream/EdgeMoE/BigMoMo). On Apple Silicon, NPUMoE notes "graph build time is significantly higher than execution time" and large graphs fall back to CPU. — [NPUMoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18788)
- **Thermal**: Bryngelson (M1) measured a 176 s saturating loop with flat power and throughput, no local DVFS, fixed power state across 56,527 dispatches; rail-off 0 mW idle. `cadamcat` (M5 Pro): at equal load both engines idle fans (~1350 RPM); at full load GPU fan 3100–3500 RPM vs ANE still idle, GPU sensor 1.3–3.3 °C warmer. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22283v2); [GitHub README](https://github.com/cadamcat/llms-on-apple-neural-engine)
- **Private-API fragility**: the direct route (Orion, ANEForge, Bryngelson) uses undocumented `_ANEClient`/`_ANECompiler`/Espresso APIs that break across OS updates, can carry no entitlement, and is for measurement/research only. — [Orion](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06728); [ANEForge](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17090v2)

### Inferences
- A variable-length 4-bit MoE decode/prefill on ANE will need: fixed shape buckets (per expert capacity tier and per prefill chunk), precompiled graphs, hot-swappable weights via delta reload or IOSurface adapters, and CPU fallback for router/scatter — otherwise recompilation and dispatch dominate.
- Thermal throttling is unlikely to be the blocker; correctness/compile defects, memory/disk leaks, and graph-size limits are.
- Batch/fuse at the graph level rather than chaining many per-expert dispatches.

### Gaps
- No systematic study of cold-start load time for 4-bit multi-expert ANE graphs (number of programs × compile ms) beyond Orion's 24-program/~1015 ms and ~119-compile limit.

## Q7. Hybrid execution research (splitting across ANE + GPU + CPU, scheduling/partitioning that worked)

### Takeaway
The consistent winning pattern is: ANE for static dense tensor work (attention projections, expert FFNs, softmax), CPU for control/dynamic ops (routing, top-k, scatter/gather, sampling, embeddings, optimizer), GPU left free for other work or used for long-context attention/large GEMMs. Core ML itself promises automatic blending but in practice routes inconsistently; explicit partitioning is required.

### Cited Findings
- **NPUMoE (M2 Max/M2 Ultra)** — the clearest hybrid MoE result: dense attention (QKV, softmax, KV update) and expert FFN on NPU with static shapes; layerNorm, masking, routing (top-k/argmax), dispatch/merge (scatter/gather, weighted combine) on CPU; GPU deliberately kept free for background tasks. Three techniques: offline-calibrated static expert-capacity tiers (high-demand experts get larger tier, e.g. top quartile 4× base capacity; base = chunk/E; imbalance ratio r_ℓ = max expert load / mean load), grouped expert execution (fuse 4/8/16 experts per graph with per-expert capacities 16/32/64; one launch processes all experts as padded contiguous slices, then scatter valid rows on CPU), and load-aware graph residency (hot experts' grouped graphs resident on NPU; cold/small groups to CPU to avoid dispatch cost; overflow tokens pruned by L2-norm-of-attention-output saliency). Hard limit: graph >1.2 GB on M2 Ultra falls back to CPU. Results: 1.32–5.55× lower prefill latency, 1.81–7.37× lower energy, 1.78–5.54× lower CPU cycles, <1.1% accuracy loss vs FP16; claims dataset-stable hot experts and negligible accuracy cost from pruning. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18788)
- **Orion CPU/ANE split (M4 Max)** — table 5 (see Q4); LORA adapters passed as IOSurface inputs so base weights remain compiled and adapters hot-swap with zero recompilation; delta compilation keeps training in one process. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06728)
- **Core ML's own design (Apple)** — "Core ML then seamlessly blends CPU, GPU, and ANE to create the most effective hybrid execution plan"; Apple argues for deliberately restricting implementation flexibility to harness ANE and thereby free CPU/GPU for non-ML work and avoid inter-engine context-transfer overhead. — [Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/neural-engine-transformers)
- **`AtomGradient/hybrid-ane-mlx-bench` (M2 Ultra, macOS 26.3)** — attempted CoreML-prefill + MLX-GPU-decode hybrid: TTFT 100 ms vs GPU-only 96 ms on short prompts, but 8.2× slower for Qwen3.5-9B (319 vs 39 ms) due to 4-chunk CoreML dispatch overhead and mixed-precision cache bridge (11–16% decode degradation); note CoreML routed to GPU on their OS, so this measures framework overhead, not ANE. Genuine ANE batch dispatch via private API: 268 tok/s prefill for 0.8B, 11.3× sequential. — [GitHub README](https://github.com/AtomGradient/hybrid-ane-mlx-bench)
- **ANEForge (M5 Pro)** — single hardware lane: concurrency comes from fusing work into one program, not parallel dispatch; fused 128-op programs pay the ~74 µs floor once; ResNet-18 uses ~9× less energy than GPU at matched precision. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17090v2)
- **Panaro (2023)** — on Sonoma/iOS17, separate the CPU work into its own model (CPU segments at the start of an ANE-bound Core ML model cause a severe slowdown) and use IOSurface-backed I/O. — [Blog](https://stephenpanaro.com/blog/inside-apples-2023-transformers.html)
- **EStream / BigMoMo / EdgeMoE (Snapdragon/Qualcomm, not Apple)** — complementary patterns for MoE: one compiled expert graph shared across all experts with runtime token/weight-address binding; flash-resident expert pool streamed through a fixed NPU-addressable arena; UFS–NPU pipeline overlap; speculative-decoding windows to batch expert weight movement; cache-aware lossy routing. These are directly relevant design templates if ANE shapes must remain static. — [EStream](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06551v1); [BigMoMo](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14643v2); [EdgeMoE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10906629); [MoCCE](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.00099)

### Inferences
- The evidence supports a hybrid plan of record for a 4-bit MoE on Apple Silicon: keep router/top-k/gather/embedding/sampling on CPU or GPU; compile static expert-FFN and attention-projection graphs for ANE in a small number of shape buckets (capacity tiers + chunked prefill); fuse multiple experts per graph; keep base weights compiled and adapt via IOSurface inputs/delta reload; validate quantized numerics against CPU.
- Offloading prefill to ANE mainly frees GPU (62 W→0.22 W in one report) rather than beating its latency; worth doing if GPU contention matters, not for raw speed.
- Public Core ML auto-placement cannot be trusted as the partitioning mechanism; explicit device assignment plus measurement is required.

### Gaps
- No published hybrid result specifically combining 4-bit weights + MoE expert routing + ANE decode on any Apple chip; the closest (NPUMoE) targets prefill and uses FP16.
- No public benchmarks of the newer "Core AI" (CoreAI) runtime outside the `cadamcat` repo, and no independent replication of NPUMoE's gains.
