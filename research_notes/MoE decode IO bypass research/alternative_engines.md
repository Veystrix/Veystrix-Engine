# Alternative Inference Engines That Beat SSD-Bound MoE Decode — Transferable Mechanisms for Veystrix

Scope: survey of engines/runtimes that solve SSD- or RAM-bound MoE decode, with emphasis on Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (`qwen4_exp`, 512 experts/layer, top-10+1) and 100B+ MoE on 16–64 GB. Every claim below carries an inline source. The required output format is preserved: four key questions, each with Takeaway / Cited Findings / Inferences / Gaps.

Baseline context supplied by the coordinator (not independently re-researched here): Veystrix on MLX/MLX-VLM Python, 16 GB M4 — Flash-Next 2.3 tok/s at 408 MB/token; Nemotron Lightning (128E top-6) ~7 tok/s at 436 MB/token; targets 5+/15+ with no quality loss; bit-exact C++ runner `Jakevin/moe-offload` reaches 9.0–9.6 tok/s on 64 GB and optimized Python already matched it.

---

## Q1: What mechanisms do alternative engines use to beat SSD/RAM-bound MoE decode, and what throughput do they actually report?

### Takeaway
Every successful engine converges on the same core idea — know the next expert working set *before* it is needed and get it into fast memory with I/O overlapped — but they differ sharply in *how* they obtain that lookahead (LRU temporal locality, learned prefetch, seq2seq prediction, speculative-decoding windows, or prediction-as-routing) and in whether they modify model semantics. Bit-exact approaches (llama.cpp `#27861`, 1bit.gg, FlashMoE, SeqMoE, SSD-LLaMA, BigMoMo) deliver roughly +15% to +40% on host-offloaded decode; the dramatic 5–6× memory-footprint reductions (Edge0) require replacing the router and incur a measured quality loss.

### Inferences
- The single most reliable +15–45% for Veystrix is **llama.cpp #27861's exact LRU slot cache with a zero dummy slot and summed outputs**. It needs no new GPU kernel and is therefore directly expressible in MLX (gather rows into a slot tensor, masked add of the CPU/GPU partials). The Inovello result shows it works even on PCIe 3.0 with slow DDR4 — i.e. the bandwidth regime Veystrix is in.
- **Highest-value "free" tricks**: (a) pin/lock host experts (llama.cpp `-lm mlock/dio`, #28223 — Inovello's cache needed this); (b) make each expert's bytes contiguous (SSD-LLaMA expert-packs — the HN commenter notes this may need a GGUF layout change but GGUF allows alternate layouts); (c) let macOS's unified-buffer page cache do hot-expert reuse (SwiftLM). None change numerics.
- **MTP is a force multiplier for I/O, not just compute.** 1bit.gg measured that MTP leaves reads per generated token unchanged within 0.5% while raising resident decode 24–26 → 40–49 tok/s, because a verification step routes 4 tokens at once and keeps more reads in flight. BigMoMo formalizes this. Qwen3.8-Flash-Next's native MTP head is therefore the highest-leverage existing asset in the model.
- **Predictive scheduling is the common ingredient of every big winner** (Origins AI survey). SeqMoE's 80.22% of full-load at 45% residency is the ceiling the field is approaching; achieving it requires a trained predictor plus a graph-friendly runtime, whereas LRU alone plateaus at ~85–92% hit.
- **Quality-preserving vs quality-trading split is clean**: Edge0 is the only surveyed system whose ~20 tok/s on 16–24 GB Macs requires changing routing and loses 3.9–10 benchmark points. Everything else is bit-exact but delivers more modest gains on host-offloaded decode. Veystrix's "no quality loss" requirement rules Edge0 out as a primary path.

### Gaps
- `Mference`, `ssdmoe`, `TinyGiant`, `Swap-MoE` and `exo` produced no reliable, specific MoE-offload tok/s in the searches performed. `exo` appears only in an incidental blog comment ("EXO + Mac Studio + DGX Sparks = 2.8× gains", Jan 2026). `BigMoeOnEdge` (Apache 2.0) was mentioned in a llama.cpp GenieX issue comment as doing MoE streaming through llama.cpp's public `cb_eval` API — plausible lead, unverified numbers. The "SSD MoE finally viable!" reddit thread returned no body. No conclusion should be drawn about these engines.
- Absolute tok/s figures are not comparable across rows: VRAM amount, quant, context length, KV policy and whether MTP is on vary wildly. The reader should compare *ratios* (`after/before on the same box`) rather than absolute values.
- FlashMoE's +51% hit-rate claim (on OLMoE/Qwen3-30B) conflicts with 1bit.gg's trace finding that W-TinyLFU/ARC/LRU-2 were never more than 2.4 points above shared LRU and LFU failed badly on chat. Both are primary sources; the discrepancy is likely workload/policy-detail dependent and is not resolved here.
- vLLM-metal/SGLang numbers found are dense-model serving benchmarks, not SSD/RAM MoE offload; no 16–64 GB Mac MoE-offload figure was found for them.

## Q2: What do the 1bit.gg Qwen3.8-Flash-Next measurements actually show — is "28–39 tok/s with hot-expert pinning" a real result for this model?

### Takeaway
**No — the headline needs correcting.** 1bit.gg's 28–39 tok/s streaming figures are for the *30B* Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B, not Flash-Next. Flash-Next streamed exactly runs at **3.5–6.2 tok/s** with 19–38% of experts in RAM, and 40–49 tok/s only when the full ~77 GiB is resident *with MTP n=3*. Moreover, 1bit.gg and llama.cpp #27861 both found that **static hot-expert pinning is a dead end** for this model; what works is recency-based (LRU) caching over a shared budget. The single most important design conclusion for Veystrix is that **read concurrency on a latency-bound SSD matters more than prefetch accuracy for Flash-Next.**

### Cited Findings

All figures in this section: [1bit.gg MoE streaming](https://1bit.gg/moe-streaming.html) unless otherwise noted.

- **Model math**: Flash-Next UD-Q4_K_XL is 111 GB — 71.7 GiB routed experts (24,576 experts, 3.06 MiB each), 26.8 GiB per-layer token embeddings (read lazily), 4.9 GiB rest; 48 layers × 10 routed experts = **1.47 GB of expert reads per token**. NVMe does 4.91 GB/s sequential-16MiB, 3.62 GB/s random-3MiB single, 3.87 GB/s random-3MiB×8-in-flight; uncached decode ceiling ≈2.5 tok/s.
- **Streamed, bit-exact Flash-Next** (`llama-perplexity` batch-1 over 512 tokens; KLD 0 vs resident, PPL 11.32): 4,608 slots (19%): **3.5–3.7 tok/s**, 22.0% misses, 157 GiB/512 tok; 9,216 slots (38%): **5.4–6.2 tok/s**, 9.5% misses, 68 GiB/512 tok. Resident on Vulkan with MTP n=3: **40–49 tok/s** (≈22 ms/token); resident without MTP: 25–26 tok/s (38.5 ms/token).
- **Where the 28–39 tok/s actually comes from**: Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B (48 layers × 128 experts = 6,144), Q4_K_M, 4,608 slots (75%): **28–38 tok/s streamed** (12 cold), 79–91 resident; at 3,072 slots (50%): 17–22; at 1,536 (25%): 8–10. The cache-sweep table gives 28.8–33.9 (code) and 45.3–48.4 (chat) at 13.2 GiB, and notes the all-resident rate of 93 tok/s needs 17.3 GiB. 1bit.gg's *models* page lists **Flash-Next at 24.0 tok/s decode / 287 tok/s prefill on Strix Halo HRX** with everything resident — [models page](https://1bit.gg/models.html).
- **Why static hot-expert pinning fails, confirmed twice**: PR #27861 instrumented a 54k-record mixed workload and found "no exploitable static skew: a top-32 'hot expert' list learned on half the workload covers only ~10% of the other half (uniform = 6.2%). Static pinning of experts is a dead end." What exists is strong *temporal* locality: per-layer LRU-64 ≈67%, LRU-128 ≈81% hit rate ([PR #27861](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27861); independent Blackwell trace: LRU-64 81.5%, LRU-128 90.2%, saturating ~98.5% at 384–512 slots).
- **Cache policies**: g-lru (one budget shared across all layers) is the best simple policy or within a point on every 1bit.gg trace; LFU fails on chat (Coder chat at 32/layer: 73.3% LFU vs 85.2% LRU); decayed g-lfu recovers it. Flash-Next specific hit rates at 192/layer (27.5 GiB): lru 91.7%, g-lru 92.8%, opt 96.9% (code); chat 88.7/89.9/96.4. ARC, LRU-2 and W-TinyLFU are ≤2.4 points above g-lru and up to 13 points below it — "routing has little popularity beyond recency."
- **Latency, not bandwidth, is the limiter**: with whole expert parts (~1 MiB) and 8 threads, a Flash-Next layer's misses make only 6–7 reads and each `pread` takes 4.5–4.8 ms (~1.8 GB/s) from a drive rated 3.4 GB/s at that depth. **16 threads × 256 KiB chunks** cut a 512-token pass from 174–177 s to 143–151 s (14–18% faster, perplexity unchanged); 32 threads regressed. Stressing the queue depth is worth more than prefetch: with the oracle (all of a token's reads in flight at once) speed was 10.2–11.5 tok/s vs 7.3–8.8 demand-only, "1.4–4.8× faster, from the same bytes."
- **Prefetch actively hurts Flash-Next**: the gate-ahead predictor (apply layer l+1's router to layer l's FFN input) is right ~2/3 of the time, but at 4,608 slots depth-1 prefetch took 247 s/pass vs 133 s without (240 vs 157 GiB read); depth-2 took 253 s. Recommendation: `ONEBIT_MOE_PREFETCH=0`. Root cause: Flash-Next's 4 hyper-connection residual streams mean layer l+1's router input is l+1's own mixer applied *after* layer l's MoE, so the simple predictor mixes through the wrong weights. A "next layer's own block" predictor is designed but untested on device.
- **Substitution reduces quality sharply on this model**: replacing a missing expert with the router's next choice at r=0.5 cut reads 157→64 GiB but raised KLD to 0.075 and dropped same-top-token to 90.2%; r=0.9 kept KLD 0.015–0.028 for a 5–15% gain. (On Coder-30B r=0.5 costs only KLD 0.012.) Explanation: a token spreads over 10 of 512 experts, so a stand-in differs more. 1bit.gg's recommendation: "Use r 0.9 or none on this model."
- **Host round-trip caps streamed decode**: on Coder-30B, 0.37 ms between per-layer remaps × 48 layers ≈ 17.6 ms/token ≈ 57 tok/s ceiling, vs 12.5 ms (80 tok/s) resident — *every layer's expert ids travel to the host and slot indices come back*. "Fewer host round trips" is 1bit.gg's explicit next step.
- **Per-layer co-activation placement is real but overfit**: putting co-activated experts on one device yields 44–60% of tokens that skip the wire in-sample, but only 18.9–26.3% on held-out steps and 7–13.5% cross-prompt (contiguous control 0.2–0.6%); still 15–50× the control, but "a 7–26% lever, not a 44–60% one."

### Inferences
- The coordinator's premise "1bit.gg Qwen3.8 measurement 28–39 tok/s with hot-expert pinning" should be restated in the final report as two separate facts: (i) 1bit.gg's Flash-Next *resident with MTP* = 40–49 tok/s on Strix Halo 128 GB; (ii) 1bit.gg's *streamed with ~75% of a smaller model's experts pinned* = 28–38 tok/s for Coder-30B. There is no 1bit.gg measurement of Flash-Next at 28–39 tok/s under SSD streaming.
- Veystrix's own 408 MB/token ≈ 1.47 GB/token at Q4 for the same model order-of-magnitude; 1bit.gg's drive math says a cold read path caps decode near 2–2.5 tok/s. Veystrix's 2.3 tok/s is therefore exactly the "no cache, bandwidth-bound" operating point — the entire 5+ target is reachable by cache-hit-rate engineering, not by faster kernels.
- A g-lru hit rate of ~92% at 27.5 GiB of experts would cut Flash-Next's reads from 1.47 GB/token to ~0.12 GB/token; 1bit.gg's own drive-ceiling model then predicts ~24–34 tok/s overlapped — far above Veystrix's 5+ target even with a slower Apple SSD and higher host overhead. The binding constraint on a 16 GB M4 is not the SSD, it is that there is little RAM to hold a hot set; this makes *which* 1–4 GiB to pin (recency/LRU, per-layer shared budget) the decisive design choice.
- Because prefetch makes Flash-Next *worse*, Veystrix should not copy the "gate-ahead" predictor; it should maximize in-flight demand reads (chunked 256 KiB, ~16 threads, many layers queued at once), use MTP to widen the read batch, and keep a shared-budget LRU.

### Gaps
- 1bit.gg explicitly states the streamer was removed from the engine build and its Flash-Next measurements were taken on Vulkan before removal; the HRX port sits behind `ONEBIT_MOE_STREAMING_HRX` and is untested. So the numbers are a record, not a currently reproducible default.
- No 1bit.gg measurement on Apple Silicon / M-series SSD was found; all its numbers are on Strix Halo's YMTC PC41Q NVMe. Apple SSD latency under `pread`/F_NOCACHE semantics is uncharacterized here.
- The "next layer's own block" predictor for Flash-Next is designed and host-side unit-tested but explicitly untested on device; no hit-rate or tok/s result exists.

## Q3: Which engines actually report >10 tok/s for 100B+ MoE on 16–64 GB?

### Takeaway
Three credible candidates clear the bar, plus several that do so only on ≥64 GB or with a quant/pruning change. The only 16–24 GB Mac result above 10 tok/s is **Edge0** (20.4 tok/s at 2.9 GiB active on a 24 GB M4 Pro, 35B class) — but it is not bit-exact. Bit-exact 100B+ results above 10 tok/s generally require either ≥32 GB of RAM/VRAM (llama.cpp caches, Inovello) or a 64–128 GB unified-memory Mac (1bit.gg, oMLX).

### Cited Findings

- **Edge0 — 35B-class, 24 GB Mac Mini M4 Pro: 20.4 tok/s, 2.9 GiB peak active** at K=4 vs 3.9 tok/s / 18.2 GiB fully resident; on a 16 GB M2 MacBook with an 18.4 GiB checkpoint, blocking time dropped 244.0→101.9 ms/step and decode throughput rose 82%. Quality: 3.9 pt mean gap to fp16, 6.1 pts AIME 2026 at 35B. **Not bit-exact.** [Origins AI survey](https://originshq.com/blog/moe-ssd-expert-serving-runtimes/) (secondary); primary [arXiv:2609.18063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18063); code [github.com/Edge0-AI/Edge0](https://github.com/Edge0-AI/Edge0).
- **llama.cpp #27861 + Inovello config — Flash-Next (≈125B/177B) on 2×3090 (24 GB each) + 188 GB DDR4, PCIe 3.0: 17.6→25–29 tok/s** with 80→135 cache slots; **37–41 tok/s** reported in the Part 2 follow-up with a different quant + MTP (title only retrieved). Bit-exact. [PR #27861](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27861); [Inovello writeup](https://inovello.dev/writeups/qwen3-flash-next-2x3090-expert-cache/); [Part 2](https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/1w6ozbj/update_qwen38flashnext_on_2x3090_ddr4_part_2_2529/).
- **llama.cpp #27861 on consumer 8–16 GB GPUs — Flash-Next**: single R9700 16 GB + DDR5: 12.8→18.0 tok/s with 84 slots; 2×RX 6950XT 16 GB (OCuLink, 64 GB host RAM, Vulkan): 8.2→17.4–19.1 tok/s at 256–280 slots with 92–93% hit rate; **21–24 tok/s with MTP + cache**. RTX PRO 4500 Blackwell 32.6 GB: 16.83 stock → 41.31 tuned (with q8 KV caveat). Bit-exact. All in the [#27861 thread](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27861).
- **1bit.gg — Flash-Next resident on 128 GB Strix Halo: 24.0 tok/s** (HRX, dense route), **40–49 tok/s with MTP n=3** on Vulkan (≈77 GiB resident). Bit-exact. [models](https://1bit.gg/models.html); [moe-streaming](https://1bit.gg/moe-streaming.html).
- **oMLX/MTPLX — Flash-Next derivatives on M4/M5 Max 128 GB: 48.7 tok/s (REAP-288 oQ4e, M4 Max 40c), 62.5 tok/s (512-expert Jundot, M5 Max), 95 tok/s (256-expert GSQ-RCO MLX, M5 Max), up to 125 tok/s claimed by mtplx**. Quality depends on whether experts were pruned; see [HF REAP-288 card](https://huggingface.co/mensaprodigy/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-REAP-288-oQ4e-BF16-MTP-PLE), [MLX 256E card](https://huggingface.co/mbrown-uno/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GSQ-RCO-Coder-MLX), [mtplx](https://mtplx.com/docs/run-qwen-3-8-on-a-mac/).
- **KTransformers — DeepSeek-V3 671B on 14 GB VRAM + 382 GB DRAM: 12.2–13.7 tok/s decode** (6–8 experts), 1× or 2× Xeon; bit-exact core, Expert Deferral ≤0.5% accuracy drop. [DeepSeek tutorial](https://github.com/kvcache-ai/ktransformers/blob/747dc0596c24a67dd16518d8676c6595949b1514/doc/en/DeepseekR1_V3_tutorial.md); [SOSP paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3731569.3764843).
- **SSD-LLaMA — Kimi-K2.7-Code (>1T params, 500 GB 4-bit) on 1×RTX5090 + 32 GB RAM: >1 tok/s**. Does not meet the >10 bar but is the scale leader. [Origins AI survey](https://originshq.com/blog/moe-ssd-expert-serving-runtimes/).
- **SwiftLM — 122B on M5 Pro 64 GB: no tok/s reported** (2,694 MB active VRAM, ~9 GB/s streaming claimed); not usable as evidence. [HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47604354).

### Inferences
- For Veystrix's 16 GB M4, the literature says 5+ tok/s bit-exact is plausible (Edge0's 82% gain on a 16 GB M2 and the 8.2→17–19 tok/s消费级 GPU results suggest the mechanism works even when memory is tight), but **15+ tok/s bit-exact at 16 GB has no published precedent in this survey** — it would require ~90%+ hit rates on a cache that fits in a few GiB, which the traces say needs 27+ GiB for Flash-Next. The 15+ target likely requires changing the model (pruning/MTP+KV compression as in oMLX) or accepting Edge0-style routing changes.
- The "64 GB" in the coordinator's C++ comparison (Jakevin/moe-offload 9.0–9.6 tok/s) is consistent with the #27861 consumer-GPU numbers (12.8→18 tok/s): both are host-offloaded, exact, and cache-assisted. Veystrix's optimized Python already matching the C++ runner means the remaining gap to 5+/15+ is architectural (cache + I/O + MTP), not language-level.

### Gaps
- No result found for any 100B+ MoE at >10 tok/s on exactly 16 GB unified memory that is also bit-exact. Edge0 is 24 GB (tested) and 16 GB (blocked-time measurement, no final tok/s given).
- The Inovello Part 2 "37–41 tok/s" figure could not be verified beyond its title; it also uses an unspecified "different quant," which may affect quality.

## Q4: Which mechanisms should Veystrix adopt, ranked by transferable value?

### Takeaway
Rank by (bit-exactness × expected gain × ease of port to MLX). The top tier is a shared-budget LRU slot cache with exact partial sums, expert-contiguous storage, pinned host pages, and exploiting the model's native MTP as an I/O batching window. Predictive prefetchers (SeqMoE, FlashMoE, SpecPrefetch) are second tier: high ceiling, but they need training and 1bit.gg showed prefetch can *hurt* Flash-Next. Edge0 is a last-resort, quality-trading option.

### Cited Findings — ranked list with the enabling source

1. **Exact LRU slot cache with zero dummy slot + summed partials** — [llama.cpp #27861](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27861). +31% on 2×3090, +15–40% on single GPUs; no new kernels; bit-exact. Caveats from the PR thread that matter for a port: (a) duplicates of the dummy slot break CUDA batched `mul_mat_id` above 8 tokens — the clean fix is **n_expert_used dummy slots** (10 for this model), so give the cache 10 distinct zero slots; (b) MTP verification batches (>1 token) need a small extension of the remap and are worth ~+20–30% on top; (c) freeze/double-buffer the id→slot tables during multi-ubatch prefill to avoid torn partitions; (d) warming the LRU from prefill expert frequency helps slightly.
2. **Native MTP treated as an I/O scheduler** — [1bit.gg](https://1bit.gg/moe-streaming.html) and [BigMoMo arXiv:2609.14643](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14643). MTP leaves drive bytes/token unchanged but raises resident decode 24–26→40–49 tok/s; BigMoMo's reuse-aware admission + adaptive flash reorganization + cross-stage overlap gives 4.83× on mobile. Flash-Next already ships the MTP head.
3. **Chunked, high-depth parallel reads for demand misses; do not prefetch** — [1bit.gg](https://1bit.gg/moe-streaming.html). 16 threads × 256 KiB beats 8 threads × whole parts by 14–18%; more in-flight reads give 1.4–4.8× vs latency-bound demand reads; gate-ahead prefetch *doubles* pass time on Flash-Next.
4. **Shared-budget (g-lru) eviction with decayed-frequency admission, warmed by prefill** — [1bit.gg traces](https://1bit.gg/moe-streaming.html), [llama.cpp CRF design (PR #31286)](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/31286), [KTransformers PrefillHot](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12978v1) (PrefillHot is named as a KTransformers/FreeToken/MoE-APEX policy in the SeqMoE paper; no KT primary doc found — see Gaps). g-lru is best or within a point on all 9 traces; static hot lists provably fail (PR #27861's 54k-record study).
5. **Expert-contiguous on-SSD layout + concurrent direct reads** — [SSD-LLaMA arXiv:2609.18110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18110) and the [HN technical comment](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47604354) noting GGUF supports alternate layouts. 1.52–4.19× prefill / 2.10–15.58× decode; FlashMoE's separate-expert files give 4× faster initial load and 2.5× total vs llama.cpp.
6. **Pin/lock hot host buffers, avoid anonymous-memory repacking** — [llama.cpp docs](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/moe-cache.md), [PR #31122](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/31122), [Inovello #28223](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/28223), [SwiftLM HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47604354) (OS page cache for hot-expert reuse).
7. **Trained predictors: seq2seq Mamba2 (SeqMoE) / tiny FFN cache policy (FlashMoE) / shared advisory adapter (SpecPrefetch)** — [SeqMoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12978v1), [FlashMoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17063v1), [Origins AI survey](https://originshq.com/blog/moe-ssd-expert-serving-runtimes/). SeqMoE 80.22% of full-load at 45% residency; FlashMoE ≤113 KB model trained in ~2 h. Higher complexity, modest standalone gains on small models.
8. **Graph-friendly runtime / fewer host round-trips** — [SeqMoE §8](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12978v1), [1bit.gg "Where streamed decode spends its time"](https://1bit.gg/moe-streaming.html), [KTransformers CUDA Graph note](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3731569.3764843). 0.37 ms/layer round-trip caps Coder-30B at ~57 tok/s; CUDA Graph cut KT's GPU invocation overhead from >20% to ~0. MLX's `mx.compile`/unified buffers are the natural analogue.
9. **Last resort, quality-trading: prerouter-as-routing + unmerged LoRA (Edge0), or expert pruning (oMLX REAP/GSQ-RCO)** — [Origins AI survey](https://originshq.com/blog/moe-ssd-expert-serving-runtimes/), [oMLX cards](https://huggingface.co/mensaprodigy/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-REAP-288-oQ4e-BF16-MTP-PLE). 20.4 tok/s on 24 GB / 95 tok/s on M5 Max, but 3.9–10 pt quality gaps or a permanently pruned model — conflicts with Veystrix's "no quality loss" target.

### Inferences
- Ranked by "can be tried this week in the existing MLX Python engine without quality risk": (1) exact LRU slot cache, (3) read concurrency tuning, (4) g-lru + prefill warming, (6) buffer pinning / page-cache-friendly layout, (2) MTP. Items 5 and 8 need a C++/checkpoint change; 7 needs training; 9 changes quality.
- The most compelling single experiment for Veystrix: replicate llama.cpp #27861's **48–96 slot per-layer cache with 10 distinct zero slots** on Flash-Next in MLX, measure hit rate and tok/s, then layer MTP verification-batching on top. The Inovello result (8.5 GB of cache beating 8.5 GB of resident layers by 45% on PCIe 3.0) is the concrete benchmark to beat.

### Gaps
- **"PrefillHot"** appears only secondhand: the SeqMoE paper says "LRU, LFU, and PrefillHot are adopted by FreeToken, MoE-APEX, and KTransformers," and a GitHub issue (crow-nest #213) says "PrefillHot selects the most frequently activated experts..." No KTransformers primary doc describing PrefillHot was found. Do not attribute a specific KTransformers tok/s to PrefillHot.
- No reliable primary documentation found for `Mference`, `ssdmoe`, `TinyGiant`, `Swap-MoE`, or `BigMoeOnEdge`; no 16 GB Mac bit-exact >10 tok/s precedent; no Apple-silicon SSD `pread`/F_NOCACHE latency characterization; no reproducible SwiftLM benchmark.
- The coordinator should treat absolute tok/s across engines as non-comparable and the 1bit.gg "28–39 tok/s hot-expert pinning" framing as a misattribution (see Q2).
