# Speculative / lookahead / multi-token decoding methods that share expert reads (MoE, exact verification, 16 GB M4)

Scope: mirror-hunt for a speculative variant where **expert-read sharing actually wins** on a 16 GB Apple M4 with SSD-offloaded MoE experts. Hard constraint from the coordinator: **no quality loss — exact verification required**. Baseline facts: Qwen 512 experts/layer top-10 ≈ 408 MB/token at 2.3 tok/s; Nemotron 128 experts top-6 ≈ 436 MB/token at ~7 tok/s; route-union ratio over 2 tokens ≈ 0.79–0.83; naive depth-2 MTP was 16% **slower**. Already rejected: native MTP all depths, prompt-lookup v2, K>1 read-sharing verifier, union-ratio prefill, ANE.

## Key Question 1 — Ranked shortlist: which method is most likely to make sharing win, with measured MoE+offload numbers?

### Takeaway
The strongest exact, published fit is **SpecFit** (expert-union-aware verification-subtree selection; 1.97–2.51× over vanilla EAGLE-3 on offloaded Qwen3-30B-A3B, gains * growing as cache shrinks), followed by **DraftExpert** (1.45× avg on CPU→GPU and Flash→NPU offload with a fixed-footprint, dataloader-free draft expert and exact verification). A third, non-method but decisive result (**arXiv:2610.34440**, measured on Apple M4 Max/llama.cpp Metal) explains *why* naive depth-2 lost and gives the falsifiable gate any candidate must pass.

### Cited Findings

**Rank 1 — SpecFit (exact; training-free; target unmodified).** Selects the verification subtree by maximizing `R̂(S)/T̂(S)`, where `R̂ = 1 + Σ p_v` (draft path probabilities) and `T̂ = b_|S| + β·F(S)`, with `F(S) = Σ_l | Ê_{l,s} ∪ ⋃_{v∈S} Ê_{l,v} |` = the **per-layer union of experts** the subtree activates — explicitly a monotone submodular coverage function, so "each node pays only for the experts it adds." The target's router and expert execution are untouched; the target verifies the chosen subtree unmodified. — [SpecFit, arXiv:2610.15417](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15417)

- CNN/DailyMail, Qwen3-30B-A3B, A100 40 GB, 48 expert slots/layer: SpecFit 8.27 tok/s vs cached EAGLE-3 6.80 (**+21.6%**) and 1.77× AR; vs vanilla EAGLE-3 **1.97×**. Verifies 2.88 draft nodes instead of 7.00; commits 13.1% fewer tokens/cycle but each cycle is 28.5% shorter; **22.1% fewer experts transferred H2D per output token**; stops drafting before level 5 in 85.8% of cycles, cutting drafting time 44.9%. 78.3% of the saving is verification. — [SpecFit §4.2, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15417)
- GSM8K: 10.79 tok/s vs 10.08 (**+7.0%**), 2.29× AR, 1.72× vanilla EAGLE-3; 5.09 vs 7.00 nodes; 13.8% shorter cycles; **11.6% fewer H2D experts/token**. — [SpecFit §4.3, Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15417)
- **Gain grows under offload pressure**: 21.6% @48 slots → 24.3% @24 → 29.2% @12 → 31.6% with cache cleared; at 12 slots, 32.7% shorter cycles and **33.1% fewer experts/token**. On RTX 4090 24 GB (slower host link, 32 slots, 0.78 ms per loaded expert vs A100's 0.41 ms): 6.70 tok/s, **+10.8%** over cached EAGLE-3, 2.51× AR, **+18.1%** over vanilla EAGLE-3 without cache. — [SpecFit §4.4–4.5, Fig. 4, Table 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15417)
- Route overhead is small and robust: a simulated predictor with only **60% accuracy** still gives 2.26× vs AR (2.27× with exact routes); a linear route head is 12.6 M params ≈ 3 expert slots, ~25 MFLOP/node, est. 0.1–0.2 ms/cycle; SpecFit's exact subtree search costs median 2.82 ms/cycle (0.7% of decode time). — [SpecFit §4.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15417)
- Caveat: size-limited selection is NP-complete; the implementation uses exact enumeration over pools of ≤18 nodes, top-2 expansion, depth ≤5, K≤7. — [SpecFit §3.1, §3.3, Prop. 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15417)

**Rank 2 — DraftExpert (exact; needs offline self-distillation; explicitly built for CPU→GPU and Flash→NPU expert offload).** Trains **one lightweight accelerator-resident draft expert per layer** (fixed footprint: shared + top-1 routed + draft expert) by self-distilling residual/logit/token/**router-agreement** signals from the frozen target. Router agreement drives (a) confidence–expansion truncation and (b) async prefetch of predicted verifier experts. "In exact mode, every emitted draft token is verified by the full target MoE"; truncation/prefetch change only how many candidates are proposed and when experts move. — [DraftExpert, arXiv:2607.24434](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24434v1)

- Decode-stage tok/s vs exact AR offload: DS/RTX4090 CPU→GPU 2.19→2.99 (**1.36×**); DS/Flash→NPU 10.18→15.47 (**1.52×**); ML/RTX4090 1.94→2.50 (**1.29×**); ML/Flash→NPU 8.50→13.69 (**1.61×**); **avg 1.45×**. The training-free shared+top-r baseline is *slower than AR* on every row (e.g., 1.19, 4.80, 1.01, 3.82). — [DraftExpert Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24434v1)
- Byte mechanism (K=4): top-1 drafter touches 84 unique routed layer-experts (DS) at 22.5% acceptance; top-3 touches 244 at 45.9% but costs 1.59× top-1 latency; DraftExpert touches **88** at **85.0%** acceptance with 1.05× cost. Prefetch hit 86–88%. — [DraftExpert Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24434v1)
- Verify-side control: fixed-K verification costs **2.35× (CG) / 3.10× (MN)** one-token AR for DS; confidence+expansion truncation → 1.48×/1.70×; + prefetch → **1.25×/1.35×** (88/12% hit/waste). — [DraftExpert Table 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24434v1)
- Model detail matching the user's regime: DeepSeek-V2-Lite and Moonlight-16B-A3B, 27 layers, 64 routed + 2 shared experts, top-6; routed experts are 91.6% of weights (26.8 GiB BF16) — same "experts dominate SSD bytes" structure as Veystrix. — [DraftExpert §2, Fig. 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24434v1)

**Rank 3 — arXiv:2610.34440 "Expert Bytes, Kernel Bandwidth and the Best Draft Length" (diagnostic + precondition, not a decoding algorithm; measured on **Apple M4 Max**, 546 GB/s).** Separates bytes from bandwidth. A deduplicating kernel reads each distinct expert once per verification pass; a per-slot kernel reads it once per token. Saving `S(n) = E_slot(n)/β_slot(n) · (1 − ū(n)/r(n))`, where `ū(n)` = share of per-slot bytes a dedup kernel requests (routing-determined) and `r(n)=β_dedup/β_slot` (kernel-determined). **Deduplication is faster iff ū(n) < r(n).** — [arXiv:2610.34440 §2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34440v1)

- On llama.cpp Metal, MLX-LM and llama.cpp CUDA the **per-slot kernel has the higher effective bandwidth** in every model at n=2..8: r = 0.32–0.75. Qwen3 on Metal: r(2)=0.50, r(8)=0.35; OLMoE Metal r(2)=0.59, r(8)=0.51; gpt-oss Metal r(2)=0.37, r(8)=0.34. Re-read cost on Metal is 0.88–1.04 (no on-chip cache benefit), vs 0.18–0.34 on CUDA. — [arXiv:2610.34440 Table 1, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34440v1)
- At n=2, ū(2) is 0.72–0.83 for all four models, **above r(2) in every engine/model pair → dedup is slower at two tokens everywhere**. Crossover is 5–8 tokens per pass, or never. With a real draft model on llama.cpp Metal, **plain decoding is fastest on 33/37 MT-Bench prompts**. — [arXiv:2610.34440 abstract, §2 "Bytes against bandwidth", App. D.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34440v1)
- Reuse is real but front-loaded: layer-mean ρ(2) = 0.363 (OLMoE), 0.480 (Qwen3), 0.492 (gpt-oss); ρ(9) = 0.762/0.780/0.856. Independent-uniform baseline m/N = 0.125/0.0625/0.125. Main results are checked in Lean 4; a one-layer expert-op benchmark fits the bandwidths in **under an hour per model**, and bytes+bandwidth predict the crossover within one pass size in 10/12 configurations. — [arXiv:2610.34440 §2, §4, abstract](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34440v1)

**Rank 4 — Cohere / MoESD (exact; mechanism confirmation, not an offload algorithm).** MoESD (NeurIPS 2025 spotlight) shows MoE gains *more* than dense from SD at medium batch because low arithmetic intensity keeps verification bandwidth-bound; up to **2.29× on Qwen2-57B-A14B**, explicitly "without accuracy loss"; expected distinct experts under uniform routing is `N(1−q^n), q=1−m/N`. — [MoESD, arXiv:2505.19645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19645)

- Measured on Cohere's MoE (N=128, k=8, K=3, vLLM): step-1 expert overlap **0.381** vs independence 0.118 and uniform 0.0625; step-4 still 0.299. Unique experts for 4 tokens: empirical **20.36** vs independence 25.4 vs uniform 29.1 (**20–31% fewer**). T(4)/T(1) = 1.25 empirical vs 1.46 Amdahl; observed SD speedup at BS=1 **1.95×** (AL=2.73). A separate mechanism at BS=1 is fixed-overhead amortization of non-expert kernels (2–10 µs, 2–3 µs is launch), and the routed-expert fraction f is only 0.30–0.55 because of shared experts. Overlap 0.377–0.385 across 13 Spec-Bench categories and 7 languages → structural, not workload-specific. — [Cohere, "Why MoE models get more from speculative decoding"](https://cohere.com/blog/mixture-of-experts-models-get-more-from-speculative-decoding)

**Rank 5 — BigMoMo (fastest Flash-offload numbers, but expert pruning makes it NON-exact as published).** Mobile Flash-offload runtime that uses the multi-token verification window as a **data-movement decoupling** opportunity: reuse-friendly expert movement, contiguous on-flash reads (hypergraph co-loading + anchor regions), and cross-hierarchy/NPU scheduling with VTCM double buffering. Mean **4.83×** over on-demand AR and **1.82×** over the best speculative MoE baseline (SpecMoEOff/SP-MoE/MoE-SpAc), 4 models × 5 benchmarks, OnePlus 15 (16 GB DRAM, 11.52 GB available, UFS 4.1), Qwen3-30B-A3B 128 experts top-8, draft length 2 / width 2. — [BigMoMo, arXiv:2609.14643](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14643v1)

- Direct byte evidence: nodes that do not contribute accepted tokens account for **82.1% of target verification compute and 81.8% of expert movement time**; expert-access requests grow *more slowly than draft length* across all models. — [BigMoMo §3.4, §4.2, Fig. 5/7](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14643v1)
- **Exactness break**: acceptance-aware *node* pruning is exact, but hierarchy-aware *expert* pruning removes routed experts and redistributes their weight to the nearest-scoring retained expert — "expert pruning changes the expert mixture used during target verification and therefore introduces an approximation to the target computation." — [BigMoMo §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14643v1)

**Rank 6 — S2-MoE (best headline on 16 GB + SSD, but explicitly lossy).** llama.cpp, self-speculative (expert-sparsity draft, top-k=1..3), routing-aware adaptive expansion with a hardware-calibrated marginal-expert cost `ΔĈ^ver(i|S)=|Ê_i \ ⋃_{j∈S} Ê_j|·T_exp`, reuse-aware expert gating, and shared-KV context alignment. **1.3–5.3×** on Jetson Orin (16/32/64 GB, SSD offload), 1.2–2.9× on RTX 4090. — [S2-MoE, arXiv:2608.15018](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15018v1)

- Jetson Orin NX **16 GB SSD-offloaded Qwen3**: HumanEval 0.47→1.22 tok/s (2.60×), math 0.41→1.30 (3.17×), MT 0.48→1.01 (2.10×), QA 0.50→1.13 (2.26×); EAGLE-3 on the same rows 0.89/0.61/0.62/0.78. Cost model R²=0.943–0.999, MAPE 0.8–4.5%. Reuse gating lowers reuse ratio 0.6017→0.4453 (HumanEval) and 0.5893→0.3943 (GSM8K). Context alignment raises acceptance up to +79% (OLMoE GSM8K). — [S2-MoE §4.1–4.3, §6, Tables 3–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15018v1)
- **Exactness break (explicit)**: "Reuse-aware expert gating intentionally introduces a bounded routing bias … and is therefore a **controlled target-routing approximation rather than a strictly lossless transformation**." PPL ratio 1.012–1.013, Top-1 agreement 89–93%; mean KL 0.026–0.065. The other two components (adaptive expansion, context alignment) are compatible with exact verification. — [S2-MoE §6.2.2, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15018v1)

**Rank 7 — MoE-Spec (expert budgeting; non-exact as published).** Training-free verification-time expert budgeting that decouples speculation depth from memory cost by enforcing a fixed expert cap; excluded experts are handled by truncation or substitution, so the target computation is altered. Reported **15–45% throughput improvement over EAGLE**. — [MoE-Spec, OpenReview BnqJcRBl9D](https://openreview.net/forum?id=BnqJcRBl9D); [NASA ADS record, arXiv:2602.16052](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260216052M/abstract); described as altering target computation in [SpecFit §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15417)

**Rank 8–11 — general methods (exact where noted, but NO published MoE+offload numbers).** EAGLE-3 itself is lossless (target weights untouched, strict speculative-sampling acceptance) with 3.0–6.5× on dense GPU targets and ~1.4× over EAGLE-2, but on offloaded MoE without a cache vanilla EAGLE-3 is *slower than AR* (0.90×) — so it is the **substrate**, not the fix. — [EAGLE-3, arXiv:2503.01840](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01840v1); [SpecFit §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15417). Dense-reference speedups: Medusa 2.12×, Lookahead 1.62×, PLD 1.74×, standard SD 1.92× (Vicuna-13B, temp 0); Medusa "relax acceptance conditions under non-greedy settings, which do not guarantee lossless acceleration." — [EAGLE-3 Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01840v1). TriForce is lossless hierarchical/long-context SD: 2.31× A100 on-chip, 7.78× on 2× RTX 4090 with offloading for Llama2-7B-128K — but KV/long-context offload, not MoE expert offload. — [TriForce, arXiv:2404.11912](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11912). MonoMoE is a weight-major fused mega-kernel for quantized MoE decode (up to 1.54× over vLLM Triton Grouped GEMM on H200; token-major kernels underuse bandwidth when few tokens reach each expert) — kernel-level, no speculative/offload numbers, orthogonal. — [MonoMoE, arXiv:2609.04244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04244). SpecMD is an Apple benchmarking framework for speculative expert *prefetching*: Least-Stale eviction cuts collision misses up to 85× vs LRU — caching/prefetch layer, orthogonal to verification math. — [SpecMD, arXiv:2602.03921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03921); [Apple ML research page](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/specmd-expert-prefetching)

### Inferences

- The shortlist splits cleanly into three levers: **(a) verify fewer/cheaper tokens** (SpecFit, S2-MoE adaptive expansion, DraftExpert truncation), **(b) make the drafter's bytes nearly free and reuse the target's loaded experts** (DraftExpert shared+top-1+resident expert; S2-MoE shared KV), and **(c) move the shared bytes once, contiguously, ahead of need** (DraftExpert prefetch, BigMoMo co-location/scheduling, SpecMD). Only (a) and (b) are exact as published.
- SpecFit's measured behavior is the closest analogue to the user's failure mode: vanilla EAGLE-3 verifies 7 nodes and is slower than AR when the cache is cleared; SpecFit verifies ~2–5 nodes chosen by *expert-union marginal cost* and wins by 18–32%. That is precisely "make sharing win by not verifying tokens whose experts aren't already shared."
- SpecFit's gain increasing as cache shrinks (21.6%→31.6%) and on a slower host link (+18.1% on RTX 4090) suggests a 16 GB M4 with SSD would be *more* favorable, not less — provided the route-union estimator is available cheaply. Its 60%-accuracy robustness result makes a small learned router head feasible within 16 GB (≈3 expert slots).
- DraftExpert's numbers are the best direct evidence that "draft models that reuse the target's loaded experts" can beat AR under real offload: training-free top-r drafting *loses* to AR, while a fixed-footprint resident draft expert with 85% acceptance and 88 touches (vs 84 for top-1) *wins* by 1.29–1.61×.
- The 2610.34440 result implies the user's ~0.79–0.83 two-token union ratio is not sufficient by itself: at n=2, Qwen3-on-Metal has ū(2)≈0.77 > r(2)=0.50. Sharing only pays at n≈5–8 (where ū drops toward r) or when the kernel/engine actually dedups at the expert level. This is a falsifiable, hardware-specific gate, not a literature guess.

### Gaps

- No paper reports exact-verification speculative MoE with expert-read sharing on **Apple M4 16 GB with SSD-offloaded experts** for Qwen-512-expert/top-10 or Nemotron-128/top-6. The closest hardware match (2610.34440, Apple M4 Max) uses **unified memory, no SSD offload** and N=128/top-8; the closest offload match (S2-MoE, 16 GB Jetson + SSD) is lossy.
- Whether Veystrix's current engine uses a per-slot or deduplicating expert kernel on Metal is unknown; 2610.34440 shows stock llama.cpp Metal, MLX-LM and llama.cpp CUDA are all per-slot on short verification passes, so the union saving may be entirely unrealized until the kernel dedups. This must be measured, not assumed.
- MoE-Spec's full text could not be retrieved (OpenReview bot check); the 15–45% figure comes from the abstract/snippet and SpecFit's related-work description only. Its exactness characterization ("truncation or substitution") comes from secondary sources.
- SpecMD was only read at abstract/snippet level; no end-to-end tok/s MoE+offload number was confirmed.
- ReDrafter, SpecInfer, Medusa/Lookahead/ReDrafter tree variants and token-tree reuse have **no published MoE+offload numbers** found; TriForce's 7.78× is KV/long-context offload, not expert offload. No evidence they address the 408–436 MB/token SSD-byte problem.
- "Certainty-based early accept" appears only as component-level confidence thresholds inside DraftExpert/S2-MoE/BigMoMo, not as a standalone validated method here.
- No published measurement of the acceptance rate of EAGLE-3-style drafters on the user's specific Qwen-512/top-10 and Nemotron-128/top-6 checkpoints; acceptance is the multiplier that decides whether n=5–8 is reachable.

## Key Question 2 — Why did naive depth-2 lose, and what would make any sharing method win?

### Takeaway
Depth-2 pays the full per-slot expert bytes of two tokens (no dedup), adds a route-prediction + subtree-selection + verification overhead on the critical path, and at n=2 the dedup byte saving is theoretically negative on Metal. Winning requires either a larger verified pass (n≈5–8), a selector that drops low-value high-fan-out nodes, a drafter that reuses already-loaded experts, or prefetch/co-location that hides the shared bytes.

### Cited Findings

- Deduplication saving is `S(n) = E_slot/β_slot · (1 − ū(n)/r(n))`; at n=2 on Metal, ū(2)=0.72–0.83 vs r(2)=0.37–0.59, so `1 − ū/r < 0` → **dedup is strictly slower**; the per-slot kernel's higher bandwidth is partly on-chip cache reuse on CUDA/MLX-LM (re-read cost 0.18–0.40) but on Metal is intrinsic to the dedup kernel (re-read cost 0.88–1.04). — [arXiv:2610.34440 §2–3, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34440v1)
- Stock short-pass kernels multiply each token by each selected expert separately, so each drafted token adds the full expert bytes of one more token even when routes overlap; reuse lowers bytes only on a deduplicating engine. — [arXiv:2610.34440 §1–2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34440v1)
- SpecFit directly quantifies the overhead trap: vanilla EAGLE-3 verifies 7 nodes, accepts only 1.54, takes 371.7 ms/cycle with cache and is slower than AR without cache (0.90×); the selector itself costs 1.43–2.82 ms/cycle but removes 28.5% of cycle time by pruning nodes with low reward-per-expert. — [SpecFit §4.2, Tables 1–2, §4.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15417)
- S2-MoE's Figure 2(b,c) shows draft confidence is concentrated near 1.0 for retained tokens (weakly discriminative) while their verification costs differ substantially, and a large fraction of verification cost comes from experts triggered by ultimately **rejected** tokens; its calibrated cost model tracks measured latency with R²=0.943–0.999. — [S2-MoE §3, §4.1, Fig. 2/6](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15018v1)
- At BS=1, fixed-overhead amortization of non-expert kernels is a distinct, large gain (T(4)/T(1)=1.25 empirical vs 1.46 from expert-only Amdahl), so a method that merely reduces expert bytes can still miss the real bottleneck. — [Cohere blog, "Demystifying the high speedup at BS=1"](https://cohere.com/blog/mixture-of-experts-models-get-more-from-speculative-decoding)
- Arithmetic intensity `AI = T(k+S)/(N+S)` explains the non-monotonic speedup-vs-batch curve; sparser models (low k/N) stay bandwidth-bound longer. For a fixed target BS, k/N and shared-vs-routed ratio determine whether verification is nearly free. — [Cohere blog, "Why MoE creates a sweet spot"](https://cohere.com/blog/mixture-of-experts-models-get-more-from-speculative-decoding); [MoESD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19645)

### Inferences

- The user's 0.79–0.83 union ratio across 2 tokens corresponds to ~8.4–8.9 unique experts per token-pair for top-10 — consistent with Cohere's empirical 0.381 step-1 overlap on 128/top-8 and with 2610.34440's ρ(2)=0.48 on Qwen3. The overlap is real; the problem is that at n=2 the *kernel bandwidth ratio* r(2)≈0.5 wipes out the ~0.8 byte share.
- Therefore a "depth-2-only" sharing scheme is theoretically dead on Metal unless (i) the kernel dedups with near-equal bandwidth, or (ii) the shared expert is read from SSD once into a local cache and reused via cache hits (which would change the effective β_slot for repeats). The M4's wide 546 GB/s unified memory and SSD latency make r(n) likely even less favorable than the M4 Max numbers cited.
- The realistic win conditions, in order: (1) **push to n=5–8** with a high-acceptance drafter so ū(n)<r(n); (2) **choose tokens by marginal expert cost** so you commit more tokens per unique expert (SpecFit); (3) **prefetch/co-locate** the shared experts so the SSD reads overlap and become contiguous (BigMoMo/DraftExpert/SpecMD); (4) **share the KV context** so draft error doesn't accumulate and acceptance stays high (S2-MoE context alignment, +24–79% acceptance).

### Gaps

- No measured r(n) or ū(n) for a 512-expert/top-10 Qwen or 128/top-6 Nemotron on a 16 GB M4; the crossover must be measured for the exact model/kernel pair.
- No published comparison isolating "dedup kernel" vs "expert cache hit" as the mechanism of byte savings under SSD (not DRAM) offload.
- Unknown whether increasing n to 5–8 is achievable on the user's checkpoints with any available drafter (EAGLE-3 acceptance on these exact models is unpublished).

## Key Question 3 — Exactness triage, 16 GB feasibility, and falsifiable tests

### Takeaway
Under the coordinator's "EXACT verification required" rule, keep only SpecFit, DraftExpert, EAGLE-3-family drafting, prefetch/caching layers (SpecMD), and the *node-pruning + context-alignment* parts of S2-MoE/BigMoMo. Discard S2-MoE reuse-aware gating, BigMoMo expert pruning/substitution, MoE-Spec budgeting, and Medusa-at-temp>0. All surviving components fit in 16 GB.

### Cited Findings

- Exact: SpecFit (target router/execution untouched), DraftExpert (exact mode), EAGLE-3 (strict speculative sampling, target weights unmodified), MoESD/Cohere analysis, TriForce, SpecMD (prefetch only). — [SpecFit](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15417); [DraftExpert](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24434v1); [EAGLE-3 §2.1/Metrics](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01840v1); [TriForce](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11912); [SpecMD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03921)
- Not exact: S2-MoE reuse-aware gating ("controlled target-routing approximation"; PPL 1.012–1.013, Top-1 89–93%); BigMoMo hierarchy-aware expert pruning + weight reassignment; MoE-Spec truncation/substitution; Medusa at temperature ≠0. — [S2-MoE §6.2.2/Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15018v1); [BigMoMo §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14643v1); [SpecFit §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15417); [EAGLE-3 Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01840v1)
- Memory overheads measured/small: EAGLE-3 drafter is a small decoder layer; SpecFit route head 12.6 M params ≈ 3 expert slots, 0.1–0.2 ms/cycle; DraftExpert adds one small expert per layer; S2-MoE runs the whole scheme inside llama.cpp on a 16 GB Jetson with SSD offload. — [SpecFit §4.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15417); [DraftExpert §3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24434v1); [S2-MoE §5/6](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15018v1)

### Inferences — falsifiable tests (ranked)

1. **Gate (run first, <1 h): measure r(n) and ū(n) on the target M4 engine** using the one-layer expert-op benchmark and routing traces from 2610.34440. Prediction: at n=2, ū(2)≈0.8 > r(2)≈0.5 → sharing cannot win at depth 2; find the crossover n* where ū(n*)<r(n*). Falsifier: if measured r(2)>0.8 on Veystrix's kernel, depth-2 dedup could win and the whole analysis changes. — [arXiv:2610.34440 §2, App. D](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34440v1)
2. **SpecFit replication (training-free):** on the existing EAGLE-3-style draft pool, replace "top-p nodes" with exact maximization of `(1+Σp_v)/(b_|S|+β·F(S))` over ancestor-closed subtrees (≤18 nodes, K≤7), with β fitted once by nonnegative least squares to cycle times. Predictions from the paper: verify ~2–5 nodes instead of 7, cut ms/cycle 14–32%, cut H2D experts/token 12–22%, and gain more as the expert cache shrinks. Falsifier: if ms/cycle does not fall by more than committed tokens fall, the trade is wrong on this model. — [SpecFit §3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15417)
3. **DraftExpert replication (needs training):** train one small per-layer draft expert with residual+logit/token+router-agreement distillation; compare fixed-K vs confidence+expansion truncation vs +prefetch. Predictions: acceptance ~0.84–0.87, verifier touches ~one top-1 expert's worth, verify cost from ~2.35× AR down to ~1.25× AR, end-to-end ≥1.3× under offload. Falsifier: if top-r drafting already reaches 85% acceptance at ≤1.05× cost, the trained expert adds nothing. — [DraftExpert Tables 2–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24434v1)
4. **Ablate the lossy bits:** for S2-MoE, disable reuse-aware gating and keep routing-aware expansion + context-aligned self-speculation; require bit-exact match to unmodified target. Prediction: accept-rate gains (+24–79%) survive; the 26–33% reuse-ratio improvement disappears, so any residual speedup must come from cheaper verification selection, not gating. Falsifier: all speedup vanishes without gating → discard. — [S2-MoE Tables 2–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15018v1)
5. **BigMoMo salvage:** keep acceptance-aware node pruning + on-flash co-loading organization + cross-hierarchy scheduling, disable expert pruning/weight reassignment. Prediction: most of the 4.83×/1.82× gain is from movement decoupling and node pruning (82% of verify cost is rejected-node work), with an exactness-preserving subset still >1×. — [BigMoMo §4.2, §5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14643v1)
6. **n-sweep with acceptance control:** measure tok/s vs draft length n=2,4,6,8 with the same drafter and a deduplicating kernel. Prediction from 2610.34440: no gain or loss at n=2, crossover at 5–8 *if* acceptance stays high; plain decoding wins on most prompts if acceptance collapses. Falsifier: monotonic gains from n=2 would contradict the measured bandwidth ordering. — [arXiv:2610.34440 abstract, §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34440v1)

### Gaps

- No end-to-end falsifiable test has been run on Veystrix hardware; all predictions above are transfers from A100/RTX4090/Jetson/phone/M4-Max measurements.
- No published byte-reduction figure for a 512-expert/top-10 model at any draft length; 408 MB/token is from the coordinator's context, not a cited paper.
- No confirmation that "exact verification" in the user's sense (bit-identical greedy output) is what the papers mean by "lossless": EAGLE-3/MoESD/TriForce preserve the sampling distribution, not necessarily bitwise greedy paths under different kernels — worth checking during replication.
